## Related Work

**Attribution and citation quality.** Document-level citations from web-enabled assistants often fail to support the accompanying text. A human audit of four generative search engines found that only 51.5% of generated sentences were fully supported by their citations and only 74.5% of citations supported their associated sentence [1]; on ELI5, even the best models in the ALCE benchmark lack complete citation support 50% of the time [2]. The AIS framework formalises when a statement is attributable to an identified source [3], and AttributionBench shows that automating this judgment remains hard, with a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reaching only about 80% macro-F1 [4]. We measure citation support in this sense, but per atomic claim bound to a verbatim quote rather than per sentence or answer.

**Claim-level factuality and verification.** FActScore decomposes long-form text into atomic facts and reports the fraction supported by a knowledge source [5]; SAFE checks each fact against search results with an LLM and agrees with crowdsourced annotators on 72% of facts [6]. Our claim labels extend the verdicts of fact-checking datasets: Supported, Refuted and NotEnoughInfo in FEVER [7], plus "Conflicting Evidence/Cherrypicking" in the web-evidence dataset AVeriTeC [8]. Other work verifies or repairs outputs: RARR searches for evidence and post-edits unsupported content [9], Chain-of-Verification plans and independently answers verification questions before revising [10], and Self-RAG trains a model to critique its own retrieval and generation [11]. Our ablation asks whether prompted self-verification alone matches external, deterministic checks. Kalai et al. argue that hallucination persists because training and evaluation reward guessing over acknowledging uncertainty [12], motivating explicit uncertainty in our outputs. Since strong LLM judges exceed 80% agreement with human preferences yet show position and verbosity biases [13], we complement our LLM judge with a human audit.

**Retrieval poisoning and source reliability.** Injecting LLM-generated misinformation into the retrieval corpus degraded open-domain QA performance by up to 87% [14], and PoisonedRAG reports a 90% attack success rate with five malicious texts per target question in a database of millions [15]. LLMs can be highly receptive to coherent, convincing external evidence that conflicts with their parametric knowledge [16], and retrieved content can also carry adversarial instructions [17]. For source reliability, six sets of expert news-domain ratings largely agree and can be aggregated to cover 11,520 domains [18], but such lists target news outlets rather than the retailer, review and forum pages typical of consumer decisions. Community Notes (formerly Birdwatch) selects crowd annotations with a bridging algorithm; users shown them were significantly less likely to reshare annotated posts [19].

**Provenance and tamper-evident infrastructure.** C2PA binds signed provenance manifests to digital assets through hard bindings, cryptographic hashes of all or part of an asset [20]. Certificate Transparency keeps publicly auditable, append-only Merkle-tree logs so that misissued certificates can be detected [21], and earlier secure audit logs use hash chains so that past entries cannot be undetectably modified [22]. The OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions standardise spans, metrics and events for generative-AI clients [23] but provide no tamper evidence. Forethought's design sketches propose "provenance tracing" and "reliability tracking" for collective epistemics [24], and related essays stress helping society appropriately trust AI-discovered information [25] while warning of "trust lock-in" and recommending auditable infrastructure [26].

**Gap.** Prior work mostly evaluates attribution and factuality offline [1–6], tests verification methods on QA and generation benchmarks [9–11], or demonstrates poisoning attacks [14–17]; provenance standards [20–23] are seldom connected to an agent's reasoning. To our knowledge, no study has measured, in end-user decision support with a fixed source pool, whether a claim-level verification and provenance layer changes what an agent asserts and recommends, including whether it adopts a planted, credible-looking falsehood, relative to citation-attaching RAG and a prompted self-verification ablation.

**Recommendation: Wealthfront, with one check before you fund it.**

At $5,000, fees and tax-loss harvesting (TLH) pull in different directions. Wealthfront is the best fit on balance.

**Why Wealthfront**
- It charges a flat 0.25% with no monthly minimum fee [2][7]. That is about $12.50 a year on $5,000 [7]. The minimum to open is $500 [2][7].
- Several sources say it harvests losses on ordinary accounts, not just large ones. One calls its TLH "daily" and says direct indexing is added at $100,000+ [2]. Another says TLH applies to all accounts [4].
- Free extras include the Path planning tool and 529 plans [2][7].

**Where the sources conflict**
- **Wealthfront TLH.** One source says automated TLH only starts at $100,000, so a $5,000 account would get none [7]. Another says its direct-indexing products unlock at $5,000 [6]. A third says direct indexing is for accounts over $100,000 [5]. The evidence is mixed, so I'm only moderately confident. Confirm TLH eligibility on Wealthfront's own site before funding. I lean toward the sources saying TLH is available because more of them agree, but I can't settle it from these pages.
- **Schwab TLH.** Source [7] ranks Schwab first and says it offers TLH from the $5,000 minimum. NerdWallet says TLH needs $50,000 [1], and another source agrees [2]. NerdWallet is more credible because it is a dated, structured review that compares platforms [1]. Source [7] looks like the outlier, so I wouldn't rely on its ranking.
- **Schwab cash drag.** Schwab charges no advisory fee, but it holds cash. Estimates range from 6–10% [2], to 6–30% [4], to about 2% [7]. Because sources disagree this widely, I can't say what the real cost is. The $5,000 minimum is consistent across sources [2][4][5][7].

**Alternatives**
- **Betterment.** This is the strongest runner-up. Its TLH is documented on taxable accounts at every balance level [2][7], and the minimum is $10 [1]. The catch is the fee. Under roughly $20,000–$25,000 it charges about $4–5 a month [2][7][9]. On $5,000 that is roughly 1.2% a year [7]. If you will add money quickly, or value its goal-based tools, it is reasonable.
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor.** It costs about 0.15% and needs $100 to open [1][2]. Sources disagree on whether it does TLH. NerdWallet lists it among TLH robo-advisors [1], while others say it doesn't [2][7]. It is a sensible choice for a Roth IRA, where TLH doesn't apply anyway [2][7].
- **Robinhood Strategies.** It lists a $3,000 TLH minimum and a 0.25% fee [1]. I only have a partial listing for it, so treat it as something to investigate.
- **Fidelity Go.** NerdWallet says TLH requires $25,000 [1], so it doesn't fit here.

**Caveats**
- TLH only helps in taxable accounts, not IRAs [2][7]. If this money is going into a Roth IRA, TLH shouldn't drive your choice, and low fees matter more.
- Most of these sources are blogs or affiliate-style pages. Only NerdWallet [1] is a major publisher, and its Wealthfront section was cut off in my copy.
- Fees and minimums change, so check each provider's current pricing.

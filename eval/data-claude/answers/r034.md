**Recommendation: Wealthfront, with Betterment as the close alternative.**

**Why Wealthfront.** It charges a flat 0.25% with no monthly minimum fee, and the minimum is $500 [2]. On $5,000 that is about $12.50 a year [7]. Betterment's Digital plan is 0.25% or $5 a month [1]. Under roughly $20,000–$25,000 the flat monthly fee applies instead [2][7]. On $5,000 that is about $60 a year, or roughly 1.2% of your balance [7]. One reviewer calls this "brutal" at small balances [2].

Wealthfront is also NerdWallet's top overall pick [2]. It has a free Path planning tool that links to outside accounts and automated 529 plans [2][7]. NerdWallet singles out Wealthfront and Public.com as the platforms offering direct indexing at low minimums [1]. One source says Wealthfront's direct-indexing products unlock at $5,000 [6].

**Caveat on tax features.** The sources disagree here. Several describe Wealthfront's tax-loss harvesting as strong and available on accounts generally [2][4]. Others say the sophisticated stock-level version needs $100,000 [2][6]. One says automated harvesting doesn't start until $100,000 [7]. Check Wealthfront's current terms before you fund the account. Harvesting only helps in taxable accounts, not an IRA or Roth IRA [7].

**Alternatives**
- **Betterment** is the better pick if tax-loss harvesting from day one matters most. It offers harvesting on taxable accounts at every balance and has a $0 minimum [2][7]. Its TLH+ feature coordinates IRA and taxable accounts to avoid wash sales [2][7]. It also has good goal-based tools [2]. Choose it if you will add money quickly and grow past the flat-fee range.
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios** has no advisory fee, but you still pay ETF expense ratios [5]. Its $5,000 minimum is exactly your budget [2]. The cost is a forced cash allocation, which is the real "fee." Estimates range from about 2% [7] to 6–10% [2], and one source says up to 30% [4]. NerdWallet lists its tax-loss harvesting minimum at $50,000 [1], and another source agrees [2]. One source says harvesting is available from $5,000 [7]. NerdWallet's figure is the more credible one, so don't count on harvesting there.
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor** costs about 0.15% net and needs $100 to open. It has no tax-loss harvesting, so it suits a Roth IRA [2][7].
- **Fidelity Go** is reported as $0 under $10,000 and 0.35% above [5]. NerdWallet lists its harvesting minimum at $25,000 [1].
- **Acorns** charges flat monthly fees that weigh heavily on small balances [3][7].

**Practical steps.** First decide whether the money goes in a taxable account or a Roth IRA, because harvesting does nothing in the IRA [7]. If it's an IRA, Vanguard's lower fee likely wins. If it's taxable and fees come first, Wealthfront's flat 0.25% is the cheapest predictable option for a $5,000 balance. Set up automatic deposits either way [7].

Some of the sources are low-quality blogs with conflicting numbers, so verify current fees directly with each provider.

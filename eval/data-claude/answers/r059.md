**Recommendation: a certified pre-owned Trek Allant+ 7, with the Ride1Up Turris as the new-bike fallback.**

**Why the Trek.** It is the only option in the sources that covers all three of your criteria.
- **Hills:** It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive motor with 85 Nm of torque, which the source says "pulls cleanly" on hills [7]. A second source says mid-drives handle hills better than hub motors, although it also says they cost more and add drivetrain wear [8].
- **Brakes:** Hydraulic disc brakes are standard [7].
- **Battery:** The 500Wh removable battery is UL certified and "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [7]. Another review makes the same point [6].

**Caveats on the Trek.**
- The sellers of the pre-owned bike are the source of most of this [7], so treat it with some caution. Source [6] corroborates the motor, battery and availability details but not the brakes.
- Stock is unpredictable [6].
- You inherit a used battery [6].
- These are used-market listings, so confirm the price is actually under $1,500 and check the battery's condition.

**If you want a new bike: Ride1Up Turris ($1,295).**
- It has a 750W hub motor, a 672Wh battery and Tektro hydraulic discs [4][6].
- One tester says it climbs 10%+ grades [4].
- The trade-off is battery replacement. Ride1Up sells direct-to-consumer only, so a battery swap means emailing support and waiting for shipped parts [6]. Another source also notes Ride1Up has no local dealer [8].

**Where sources disagree.**
- **Rad Mission 2.** The Gradient review picks it and says it has hydraulic brakes on 180mm rotors [6]. RoostMode lists the same bike with mechanical discs [8]. I can't resolve this from the sources, and since brakes are your priority I would not choose it. Its dealer-network advantage is also weakened by Rad's bankruptcy [5][6]. Two sources call that bankruptcy a risk for warranty and parts [5][6]. I'd treat the dealer-network claim cautiously, because [6] and [8] use nearly identical wording for it, which suggests they may share an origin.
- **Lectric XP4 and XP 3.0 brakes.** Source [6] says the $999 XP4 has mechanical brakes and that hydraulics are reserved for the long-range version. Sources [1] and [5] describe hydraulic brakes on the XP4. A separate source lists the XP 3.0 with mechanical brakes [4], while another lists it with hydraulic [8]. This is unresolved. Check the exact variant before buying. Also, [4] says the XP 3.0 slows on hills above 8% grade, and [6] says the XP4 labours on sustained grades, so neither suits a hilly commute well.
- **Aventon Pace 500.3.** Sources [4] and [6] agree it has hydraulic brakes and a large bike-shop footprint behind it [6]. Both also say its 500W motor feels underpowered on real hills. Its price is about $1,399 in [4]. A 2024 source says $1,699 [9], so I'd treat that older figure as outdated. This is a reasonable choice if you want local dealer support and your hills are moderate.

**Weak spots.**
- Most of these are reviews with commercial ties. Source [1] earns affiliate commissions, and the Upway posts [3][7] sell pre-owned bikes.
- Source [4] has no named author or publisher.
- None of the sources tested any of these bikes specifically on a 10-mile hilly commute, except [6], and that review's picks partly conflict with the others.

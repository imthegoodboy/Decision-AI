**Recommendation: Marcus by Goldman Sachs High-Yield Savings**, with Vio Bank as the main higher-rate alternative.

**Why Marcus.** Your priorities are no monthly fees, FDIC insurance and easy transfers, and Marcus fits the first and third best. CBS News describes it as having no monthly fee, no minimum opening deposit, no minimum balance to earn the full APY, and no cap on withdrawals or transfers [2]. BankBonus adds that you can link external accounts, that it allows same-day transfers up to $100,000, and that customer service is available 24/7 [4]. For a $15,000 emergency fund, that means no conditions to meet and no penalty for pulling money out when you need it. The rate was 3.50% APY in CBS's May 2026 comparison [2]. Savings rates are variable and can change at any time [2].

**Trade-offs.** Marcus has no checking account and no ATM access, so you would move money to your regular bank to spend it [2]. It is also not the highest-paying option.

**Where the sources disagree.**
- Axos Bank's ONE savings account is the top-rate account in both CBS and Fortune, but they give different numbers. CBS lists 4.21% [2]. Fortune's table and write-up say up to 5.00%, although its own summary says "up to 4.21%" [6]. Both agree the rate drops to 1.00% if you miss monthly direct-deposit and balance requirements [2][6]. Fortune's internal inconsistency makes its figure less reliable. Either way, those requirements make Axos a poor match for a simple emergency fund.
- Fortune is dated July 2026 and CBS May 2026, so rates in both may have moved by now. Check the current APY before opening.

**Alternatives.**
- **Vio Bank:** 4.00% on all balances, no monthly fees and no activity requirements, but a $100 opening deposit and no checking account. You must transfer to an external bank to spend the money [6]. On $15,000, the gap versus Marcus's 3.50% would be roughly $75 a year if both rates held (my arithmetic).
- **EverBank:** 3.90%, no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees, no required monthly behavior, and a highly rated app [6].
- **Synchrony:** 3.40% in CBS's list, with no minimums and an optional ATM card [2]. BankBonus says it has no fees [4].
- **LendingClub:** 4.00% only if you deposit $250 or more each month, otherwise 3.00% [2][6]. That is less set-and-forget.

**Caveats.** Neither main source explicitly states that Marcus specifically is FDIC insured. CBS says the listed high-yield accounts are federally insured deposit accounts [2], and BankBonus cites $250,000 FDIC coverage for other banks [4]. Confirm FDIC status and the current APY on the bank's own site before funding. The Ally review is from 2017 and the BankBonus article's listed publication date is 2023, so I did not rely on them for current rates [4][5]. The Gerald pages are about cash advances, not savings accounts, and are not relevant [1][3]. Most claims about individual banks rest on one or two sources, so treat the comparison as a starting point.

**Recommendation: Coursera, specifically the IBM Data Analyst Professional Certificate.**

**Why**
- It teaches Python for data analysis. Coursera describes it as "Python programming focused" and says it covers Python, Jupyter notebooks, Python libraries, SQL and Excel [1]. Google's certificate on Coursera is R-focused, so it is a poorer fit if you want Python [1].
- It gives a branded certificate. A Dataquest guide says platform certificates from Harvard, Google and IBM carry strong brand recognition, but they are completion-based, not proctored exams [4]. Two other sources also favour Coursera for university- or company-backed certificates [2][6].
- It offers hands-on work. One learner describes six hands-on labs in the Excel course and a peer-reviewed final assignment in the visualisation course [7]. That is a single anecdote, and it covers Excel and Cognos rather than Python projects, so check the Python courses' syllabi yourself.

**Time caveat.** Coursera's estimate is 4 months at 10 hours a week [1]. At 5 hours a week, my own arithmetic suggests roughly 8 months. The learner in [7], who worked full time, took about a month per course. They also cite a figure that only about 12% of people finish MOOCs. Plan for a long haul.

**Where the sources disagree**
- Course count: Coursera lists 11 courses [1], while the 2024 blogger saw 9 [7]. The program has likely changed, and the official Coursera page is more current.
- Coursera versus edX: [2] says edX is best if you want broader subjects, university backing and free access. [6] says Coursera is better for career-oriented certificates and edX for academic rigour. Neither source is independent or rigorous. [1] is Coursera's own marketing, and [2] and [6] are generalist review articles. [2] and [5] are the same article, as are [1] and [3], so they are not separate confirmations.

**Alternatives**
- **Meta Data Analyst certificate (Coursera).** It uses Python with a statistical emphasis. It is shorter, at 5 courses and about 5 months at 10 hours a week [1].
- **Dataquest.** It teaches Python through hands-on projects and costs $33–49 a month. It estimates 3–6 months at 5–10 hours a week [4]. This is the closest fit to your hours and your project requirement. However, it is portfolio-based rather than a well-known credential [4], and the source is Dataquest's own blog.
- **Harvard CS50P.** It offers a free Harvard certificate, or a $219 edX verified certificate. It takes about 100–150 hours [4]. The listed topics are general Python and do not mention data analysis libraries [4], so it would be a foundation, not a data-analysis course.
- **DataCamp.** The evidence here is thin. The only source says it is more hands-on and bite-sized, is cheaper in some ways, and is not accredited [2]. For a recognised certificate it looks weaker, but I found nothing detailed about its Python-for-data-analysis tracks.

**Bottom line.** Choose IBM on Coursera if the certificate matters most. Another option is Dataquest, because it fits 5 hours a week better and its projects give you a portfolio. [4] says hiring managers weigh portfolio projects above certificates.

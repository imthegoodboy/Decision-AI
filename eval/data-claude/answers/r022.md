I recommend DataCamp, with one caveat about its certificate that you should check yourself.

**Why DataCamp**

At about five hours a week, program length matters most, because only around 12% of people finish MOOCs [7]. DataCamp's lessons are short and run in the browser. You write Python on real datasets from the first session, which suits 30–45 minute study blocks. Its career tracks cover pandas, data cleaning, visualisation and SQL, with guided projects you can add to a portfolio [7]. Hiring managers say they care more about what you can build than what credentials you hold [4][7], so that project work counts.

**The caveat**

One recent comparison says DataCamp's certifications are now accredited and recognised for academic credit [7]. I found no second source confirming this, and an older 2024 review said DataCamp was not accredited [2]. Before paying, check DataCamp's current accreditation page. If a university- or employer-branded certificate is a hard requirement, choose Coursera instead.

**Alternatives**

- **Coursera (Python-based certificates):** Coursera's partners include Google, IBM and Meta, so its certificates are widely recognised [1][2]. Pick carefully.
  - Google's certificate teaches R, not Python [1].
  - IBM's covers Python, but Coursera estimates 4 months at 10 hours a week [1], and one reviewer puts it nearer 8 months [7]. At 5 hours a week that is likely well over a year. It also spends time on Excel and IBM's Cognos tool, which that reviewer says rarely appears in job postings [7][8].
  - Meta's is shorter, at about 5 months at 10 hours a week. It uses Python, pandas and Matplotlib, but leans toward statistics and marketing [1][7].
  - Prices differ between sources. Two older ones list Coursera Plus at $399 a year [2][6], and the newest says $599 [7]. A monthly plan keeps billing if you fall behind [7].
- **edX / Harvard CS50P:** This is a strong, free Harvard certificate, or $219 for an edX verified one [4][7]. It is a general programming course of roughly 100–150 hours, so you would still need to learn pandas and visualisation elsewhere [7]. It works well as a free supplement for fundamentals.
- **Dataquest:** Its Beginner Python for Data Analysis path costs $33–49 a month and takes 3–6 months at 5–10 hours a week. It is built on hands-on projects with real datasets, but the source describes it as portfolio-based rather than a recognised credential [4].
- **PCEP exam:** This Python Institute exam costs $69 and gives a formal entry-level Python credential [4][7].

**Suggested plan**

1. Start DataCamp's Python data-analysis track and build two or three portfolio projects.
2. Use CS50P if your programming basics are shaky.
3. If your target employers want a big-name certificate, add Coursera's Meta or IBM certificate later. Alternatively, choose it from the start if you can commit more hours.

Most of the DataCamp case comes from a single article [7]. Treat that as a reason to try a free tier or short subscription before committing to a long one.

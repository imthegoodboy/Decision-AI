**Recommendation: Wealthfront**

For $5,000 with fees and taxes as the priorities, Wealthfront looks like the best fit. It charges a flat 0.25% a year with a $500 minimum [2]. On $5,000 that is about $12.50 a year. NerdWallet's top pick for best overall robo-advisor is also Wealthfront [2]. It runs daily tax-loss harvesting, which sells losing positions to offset gains and avoids wash sales [2]. Harvesting only helps in a taxable account, not an IRA [2]. One source says Wealthfront's S&P 500 direct indexing, a stronger form of harvesting, starts at a $5,000 minimum [6]. Other sources say it starts at $100,000 [2][5], so check this before relying on it. Wealthfront also offers a free planning tool, Path [2].

**Alternatives**

- **Betterment:** This is the easiest app for beginners, with a $0 minimum, goal tracking and tax-loss harvesting at all balance levels [2]. Its fee is the problem at your size. It charges 0.25% or $5 a month [1], and the $5 option is roughly 1.2% a year on $5,000 (my calculation). One source says the flat fee applies to balances under about $20,000 to $25,000 [2][7]. Pick Betterment if you value goal buckets over the lowest cost.
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios:** It has a $0 management fee and a $5,000 minimum, which is exactly your balance [2]. But Schwab puts 6 to 10% of the portfolio in low-yield cash, which acts as a hidden fee [2]. Tax-loss harvesting requires $50,000 [1][2], so you would get no tax benefit. It makes sense mainly if you already use Schwab [4].
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor:** It costs about 0.15% a year, with a $100 minimum and a 90-day fee-free period [1][2]. It has no tax-loss harvesting [2]. That is fine if you are investing inside an IRA, where harvesting doesn't apply [2].
- **Fidelity Go:** One source lists $0 fees under $10,000 [5], which is the cheapest option here. Its harvesting requires $25,000, though [1].
- **Acorns:** It charges $3 to $12 a month [5][8] and has no tax-loss harvesting [5]. That is expensive for a small balance.

**Caveats**

Several of these sources are blogs or affiliate-style comparison pages, and they disagree on some details. NerdWallet's table [1] is the most reliable source here for fees and minimums. Tax-loss harvesting is most valuable for higher earners and larger balances. At $5,000, the savings will probably be modest [3]. Fee and promotion terms change, so confirm current pricing on each company's site before funding.

**Bottom line:** Use Wealthfront for a taxable account if tax features matter most. Use Vanguard Digital Advisor if this is retirement money in an IRA. Skip Schwab unless you already bank there.

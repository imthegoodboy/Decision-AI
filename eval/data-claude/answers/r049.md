**Recommendation: TVS iQube (a larger-battery variant), with the Ather 450X as the alternative.**

**Caveat on the evidence.** My sources don't give Bengaluru-specific service-centre counts or measured real-world range for the iQube, Ather or Chetak. The best head-to-head test is Autocar India's 2022 Mumbai comparison [1]. Treat what follows as a reasoned shortlist and check local service coverage before you buy.

**Why the iQube.** In Autocar's test, the iQube and Chetak are the easy-to-ride city commuters, and the TVS is "a step above the Bajaj" [1]. It accelerates smoothly without overwhelming the rider, and its seat is among the best for riding with a passenger [1]. It also has an Eco mode capped at 50 kph, which is about typical city pace [1]. 91Wheels lists strong build quality, a plush ride, an Eco mode that doesn't feel sluggish, and "decent range for daily use" on the bigger-battery variants [8]. Your 20 km commute is well inside the range of those variants, so range anxiety should be minor. Real-world range is always below the claimed figure, so plan on the lower number [2]. Downsides are a small 17-litre boot once the portable charger is inside [1], no fast charging, and a charger you have to carry [8].

**Alternatives**
- **Ather 450X:** It is the best-handling scooter in the test, has strong accelerator response, and keeps Eco mode usable even below 10 percent battery [1]. However, Autocar found it the least comfortable of the four, with a stiff, harsh ride on poor roads [1]. That matters on Bengaluru's potholes, so test-ride it first. It is also pricier than the iQube [8].
- **Bajaj Chetak:** It has a comfortable seat, an absorbent ride and a handy glovebox [1]. It is slower and was described as lacking features compared with rivals [1][4]. It is also the heaviest at 132 kg [4].
- **Ola S1:** I would avoid it given your priorities. Ola reported about 80,000 service complaints a month in 2024, with repair waits of 30 to 45 days and overwhelmed service centres [3][5]. The company has announced fixes such as a one-day service guarantee and 1,000 centres [7]. Those are promises from 2024, and my sources don't show whether they were delivered. Autocar also criticised its power cut-off on braking and its unusable Eco mode [1].
- **Ampere:** Ampere's own guide claims 420+ service points and LFP batteries, with the Magnus Neo at 85 km real-world range and 65 km/h top speed for 20 to 40 km commutes [2]. That is manufacturer marketing, so verify it independently.

**Before buying**
1. Test-ride the iQube and the 450X on rough roads.
2. Find the nearest authorised service centre to your home and office.
3. Ask current owners in Bengaluru about real-world range and repair turnaround.
4. Confirm the warranty terms on the exact variant you pick, since the listing I have is ambiguous [8].

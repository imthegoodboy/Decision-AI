**Recommendation: NordPass Family, with 1Password as the pick if audit evidence matters more to you than price.**

**Why NordPass.** The plan covers six separate premium accounts, with apps for iPhone, Android and Windows [7]. That fits a family of four and leaves room for two more people. It uses XChaCha20 encryption with a zero-knowledge design, so NordPass cannot read your vaults [4][7]. Three sources say its security architecture has had a third-party audit [1][7][9]. The family plan is $3.69 a month on a one-year term [1][4][7]. Sources give different two-year prices: $2.79 [1] and $2.39 [4]. NordPass raises prices at renewal, so budget for year two [1][7].

**Caveat on the audit evidence.** None of the sources names the auditor or describes what the audit found. For a buyer who ranks audits highly, that is thin. Also, [4] and [7] read like promotional or affiliate content (coupons, "our pick"). I would treat the NordPass price and audit claims as likely but unverified until you check NordPass's own pages.

**Stronger audit record: 1Password.** Its Secret Key design means a full server compromise would not expose your vault [7][9]. It publishes Cure53 penetration test results and holds SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001 [7][9]. The cost is higher. There is no free tier, Families is $5.99 a month for five people [9], and one source says it renews at $9.99 a month [7]. That renewal figure appears only in [7] and conflicts with the lower list price in [9], so check it.

**Where sources disagree on Bitwarden.** [2] says Cure53's January 2026 audit found zero critical vulnerabilities. [7] says the same audit flagged two critical issues in browser-extension autofill isolation. I give more weight to [7]. It is more specific, newer (October 2026), and the less promotional of the two. [2] also includes implausible claims, such as "AI-powered form detection with 99.2% accuracy". Even so, this is one source against one, so I'm not certain. Family plan details also conflict: $40 a year for 6 users [2], $47.88 for 6 [9], and a three-user cap [7]. Bitwarden is cheap and open source [2][5], but I would verify its plan limits before buying.

**Other options.**
- **RoboForm** is the cheapest, at about $1.59 a month for five users [1][4][7]. It has passed a third-party audit and has no reported breach in about 25 years [1][7]. [1] ranks it first, but it also says both five and six users for the family plan.
- **Keeper** is heavily certified, with SOC 2, ISO 27001 and FedRAMP High [3][9]. It costs $91.99 a year for five users [7][9], and the strongest claims about it come from Keeper's own marketing [3].
- **Avoid LastPass.** Its 2022 breach exposed encrypted vaults and unencrypted URLs [6][7][9]. The UK Information Commissioner's Office fined it £1.2 million [7][9]. A further June 2026 incident exposed customer contact data but not vaults [3][7].

**Whichever you choose,** give everyone a long, unique master password [7].

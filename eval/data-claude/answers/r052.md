**Recommendation: TVS iQube, in a larger-battery variant**

The retrieved sources are thin on hard reliability data for every brand. I chose the iQube because it fits your priorities of reliability, range and service best, and it has no major flagged weakness for a 20 km commute.

**Why the iQube**
- **Experience:** TVS and Bajaj have decades of two-wheeler experience, which Autocar credits for their well-judged ergonomics [1]. Both kept electric sales stable while Ola's service problems hurt it [4][6].
- **Build and range:** The iQube is listed with strong build quality, fit and finish. The bigger-battery variants offer decent range for daily use [9].
- **Fit for 20 km:** 20 km a day is a light load for any of these scooters. Real-world range in Bengaluru traffic runs well below the certified figures, so plan on the real number [2].
- **Ride:** Autocar found it quick to 40 km/h without being overwhelming, which suits newer EV riders. It has an indicated 80 km/h in Power mode and a usable Eco mode [1]. It also has the best seat for two-up riding [1], and the listing mentions a plush ride and 32 L of storage [9].

**Caveats**
- One source says the iQube runs out of breath at about 60 km/h on the ORR [2]. That conflicts with Autocar's 80 km/h indicated figure [1]. I'd trust Autocar's tested figure, but check the top speed on your own route.
- Regen is strong and not very natural, and there is no fast charging [1][9]. The portable charger also eats into boot space [9].
- Autocar's test was in Mumbai in 2022 [1]. None of the sources gives Bengaluru service-centre counts for TVS, so ask local owners and check dealer locations near your home and office.

**Alternatives**
- **Bajaj Chetak:** This is the comfort pick, with a wide, well-contoured seat, a glovebox and a compact charger [1]. Its indicated 69 km/h top speed feels stretched on faster roads [1], and it has a shallow boot [1]. It is a sensible choice if your commute is mostly inner-city.
- **Ather 450X:** It has the best handling and throttle response and is the second quickest [1]. It is the least comfortable, with a cramped, harsh ride for taller riders on broken roads [1], and it is the most expensive [2].
- **Ola S1 Pro:** It is the fastest and has the biggest boot [1], but I would avoid it given your service priority. Ola reportedly received about 80,000 complaints a month, with repairs taking 30 to 45 days in some cases [4]. Its HyperService promises, such as 1,000 centres and a one-day fix, are plans rather than proven results [8]. The Commute Ledger piece [2] praises Ola and puts the complaint figure at 8,000 a month, but it conflicts with other sources on that figure and on Ola's Eco-mode behaviour [1][4][8]. I would not rely on it.
- **Ampere Magnus Neo:** It is a cheaper option with 85 km real-world range and a 65 km/h top speed [3]. That source is the manufacturer's own marketing, so treat its figures cautiously.

Test-ride your top two on your actual commute before buying.

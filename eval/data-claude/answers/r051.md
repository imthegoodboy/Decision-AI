## Recommendation

I lean towards the **TVS iQube**, but only provisionally. The sources do not directly answer your top three priorities (reliability, real-world range, service network) for the iQube, Ather 450 or Bajaj Chetak. The tool's evidence-weighted ranking puts the iQube narrowly first (53/100), ahead of the Chetak (51.5) and Ather (50). Those scores are within noise of each other.

## Why

- Autocar India's Mumbai test describes the iQube and Chetak as comfortable, easy-to-ride city commuters, and puts the TVS "a step above" the Bajaj [1].
- Ola, the other mainstream option, has documented service strain. It reportedly received about 80,000 service complaints a month in 2024 [3], and the figure is repeated in [5] and [7]. That counts against it on your service-network priority.
- The iQube's claimed range was 140 km on a 4.56 kWh battery (2022 model) [4]. That is a lab-style figure. Even with a large haircut it should cover a 20 km commute, but no source gives a verified real-world number for it.

## Trade-offs

- The iQube has aggressive motor regen when you shut the throttle, which takes getting used to [1].
- None of the sources show the iQube's reliability or its Bengaluru service coverage. Having no bad reports here is not evidence of good service.
- The review data is old: Autocar's test was in 2022 and in Mumbai, not Bengaluru [1].

## Alternatives

- **Ather 450:** Best handling of the four [1], and Eco mode stays usable below 10 percent charge [1]. It is also the least comfortable, with a cramped riding position for riders over 5ft 10in [1]. Its claimed range was 146 km (Gen 3, 2022) [4].
- **Bajaj Chetak:** Comfortable [1], but Rushlane called it dated and short on features [4]. I found almost no reliability or service evidence for it.
- **Ola S1:** The Ola S1 Pro cuts power as soon as you touch the brakes, which is a problem in slow traffic [1]. Ola operates 430 service centres [3][5]. Customers reported repair waits of 30 to 45 days (anecdotal, 2024) [3]. Ola announced a plan for 1,000 centres by December 2024 [7], but the sources do not show whether it was delivered.
- **Ampere (for example the Magnus Neo):** Ampere says the Magnus Neo delivers 85 km real range against 118 km claimed, and that it has 420+ service points [2]. Both figures come from the manufacturer's own undated page, so treat them as marketing until independently confirmed.

## Conflicting evidence

I found no direct contradictions. The one tension is Ola's service problems against its announced expansion. The complaint reports [3][5][7] are corroborated across outlets. The expansion is only a plan [7], so I give the complaints more weight. All of this is 2024 data. Sources [3] and [5] largely repeat the same underlying media report, so their agreement is weaker evidence than it looks.

## Before you decide

- Check each brand's current service-centre density near your home and office in Bengaluru, and recent owner reviews. None of the sources cover this for TVS, Ather or Bajaj.
- Ask for owner-reported real range on a route like yours. Claimed figures are not real-world numbers.
- Test ride the iQube, Chetak and Ather. Ride comfort differs a lot between them [1].
- Look for 2025 or 2026 reviews, since the main comparison is from 2022.

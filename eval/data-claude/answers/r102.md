## Recommendation

A used or certified **Trek Allant+ 7** is the best fit. It ranked first in the evidence-weighted ranking (78/100), and no other option has verified claims on hills, brakes and battery availability together. The catch is that it fits under $1,500 only as used stock.

## Why

- **Hills:** It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive with 85Nm of torque, which the source says feels more natural than a rear hub motor [6]. Two other sources say mid-drives suit hills [3][7].
- **Brakes:** Hydraulic disc brakes are standard [6]. Hydraulics give more stopping power with less hand effort and self-adjust as the pads wear [7].
- **Battery:** The 500Wh battery is removable, UL certified and "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6].
- **Price:** The retailer lists it with bikes that originally cost $2,000 to $3,500 and now sell under $1,500. They are inspected and come with a one-year warranty [6].

## Trade-offs

- The Trek is used stock, so availability and condition vary, and the source does not give the Trek's exact price [6].
- Mid-drives "don't show up" among new bikes at this price [4]. That is why the best hill option here is secondhand.
- Its range (30 to 45 miles for the comparable Cannondale, and no figure for the Trek) is still ample for 10 miles. The Trek's battery is 500Wh [6].

## Alternatives

- **Ride1Up Turris ($1,295, new):** It has a 750W hub motor that reportedly climbs 10%+ grades, plus Tektro hydraulic brakes [4]. It ranked second (63/100). It is covered by only one low-reliability, undated source, and nothing in the sources verifies its replacement battery.
- **Lectric XP4 ($999):** It has a custom hydraulic brake system [1][5]. An 840Wh long-range version costs $1,299 [5]. I found no verified evidence on its hill performance or on replacement batteries.
- **Aventon Pace 500.3:** One source says its 500W motor feels underpowered on real hills with a full bag [4]. I would skip it for a hilly route.
- **Aventon Soltera.2:** Avoid it. It has mechanical brakes and a 360Wh battery, and the source says to look elsewhere for hilly routes [7].
- **Rad Power bikes:** One source flags parts and warranty risk after the company's bankruptcy [5]. Another reports a US safety regulator warning about certain Rad batteries [3].

## Conflicting evidence

- **Pace 500.3 price:** Source [4] says $1,399. A 2024 blog says $1,699 [8]. [4] is newer but less reliable, so check the current price.
- **Lectric XP 3.0 brakes:** The table in [4] says mechanical, but [4]'s own text and [7] say hydraulic. [7] is more reliable, so hydraulic is more likely.

## Before you decide

- The battery-availability claim comes from a single retailer page [6]. Confirm replacement battery price and stock with a Trek dealer.
- Check the specific used listing's condition and battery health.
- No source covers replacement battery costs for the new bikes, so ask those manufacturers directly.

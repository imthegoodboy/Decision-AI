I'm recommending the TVS iQube, but with low confidence. None of the sources gives independent real-world range or Bengaluru service-network data for the iQube, Ather, Chetak or Ola. The caveats below matter as much as the pick.

**Recommendation: TVS iQube (a larger-battery variant)**

- **Ride and build.** Autocar India's roughly 1,000 km comparison test says the iQube is aimed at comfortable, easy-to-ride city commuting and calls it "a step above" the Chetak there. It also found the iQube's acceleration won't overwhelm new EV riders [1]. 91Wheels lists strong build quality and a plush ride as pros [8].
- **Range.** 91Wheels says the bigger-battery variants "offer decent range for daily use" [8]. Another page lists a 4.56 kWh iQube with a claimed 140 km [4]. That is a claimed figure, not a measured one. A 20 km commute should fit comfortably, but I found no independent real-world test.
- **Drawbacks.** The test found the regenerative braking too aggressive. The 17-litre boot is tight once the portable charger is inside, and there is no fast charging [1][8]. The Autocar test was done in Mumbai in 2022, so it is dated and not Bengaluru-specific [1].

**Why not the others**

- **Ola S1.** This is the clearest reliability warning in the sources. Reports from 2024 describe about 80,000 service complaints a month and repair waits of 30–45 days [3]. A second article repeats the same figures, so it is probably the same underlying report and not independent confirmation [5]. Ola announced a "HyperService" plan with a one-day resolution promise [7]. That is a promise, not evidence that it worked, and none of these sources is from after 2024. Autocar also criticised the S1 Pro's power cut on braking and its very weak Eco mode [1].
- **Ather 450X/450S.** It is the best-handling scooter in the Autocar test. That test also found a stiff, harsh ride, a cramped riding position for taller riders, and a 22-litre boot [1]. 91Wheels calls the 450S's real-world range "good" [8]. No source covers Ather's service network, so that is a gap, not a strength.
- **Bajaj Chetak.** Autocar found its ride absorbent and comfortable and its seat well-shaped. It also found the boot shallow [1]. RushLane calls it dated in features [4]. I have no reliability or service evidence for it.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **Ampere versus 91Wheels.** Ampere's guide claims "strong Bangalore coverage" and 85–100+ km of real range for its own scooters [2]. It is a manufacturer's marketing page. The 91Wheels comparison lists "real-world range significantly lowered than claimed" and "service network concerns" in the column that appears to be the Ampere Nexus. The table is garbled, so I'm inferring that attribution from column order [8]. I trust the vendor page less, but both are weak, so I wouldn't treat Ampere as proven either way.
- **Ola's own numbers.** Ola's 430 service centres is a company figure, and the complaint volume is from press reports [3].

**Before you buy**

- Check how many iQube and Ather service centres are near your home and office.
- Ask local owners about repair turnaround.
- Test-ride the iQube, then the Chetak and Ather, on your own commute.

**Recommendation: a used Trek Allant+ 7 (via Upway), with caveats**

For a hilly commute with a battery you can replace, the Trek Allant+ 7 fits best. It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive motor with 85 Nm of torque, hydraulic disc brakes as standard, and a 500 Wh removable UL-certified battery that is "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6]. Upway's reviewer says the mid-drive makes a "substantial" difference on a hilly commute [6]. Mid-drives are described as feeling more natural on climbs [3][7]. A 500 Wh pack should comfortably cover 10 miles, though no source tests that route.

The main caveat is that this is a used or refurbished bike. Upway says these bikes originally cost $2,000 to $3,500 and sell there for under $1,500, with a one-year warranty and a 14-day return window [6]. Upway is a seller, and [6] is the only source that supports the Trek's battery availability, so treat that claim as lightly verified. Confirm the exact battery price and stock with Trek before buying.

**Alternatives (new bikes)**
- **Ride1Up Turris ($1,295):** [4] says its 750W motor climbs 10%+ grades and that it has Tektro hydraulic brakes. I would be cautious because [4] has no named author or publisher, and nothing I read covers replacement batteries for it.
- **Lectric XP4 ($999):** [1] and [5] describe it as having a custom hydraulic brake system and a 500 Wh pack. [5] adds that Lectric sells a larger 840 Wh version for $1,299. Neither source tests hill-climbing, and neither covers replacement batteries. It is a 71 lb folding bike with 20-inch wheels [5].
- **Aventon Pace 500.3:** [4] rates it for commuting but says its 500W motor feels underpowered on real hills with a load. That is a direct caution for your route.

**Where sources disagree**
- **XP 3.0 brakes:** [4] lists mechanical brakes in its table but describes hydraulic brakes in its own text. [7] lists hydraulic 180 mm brakes. Its battery is also given as 9.6 Ah in [4] and 10.4 Ah (499 Wh) in [7]. I trust [7] more because it is internally consistent and names a publisher, but this is unresolved. Check the spec sheet before buying.
- **Pace 500.3 price:** [4] gives $1,399, [1] says $1,000 to $1,500 (that passage is about the Soltera 2.5), and [8] gives $1,699 in a 2024 article. Prices change, so check the current one.

**Bikes to avoid**
- **Rad Power:** [5] says the company went through bankruptcy and warns that warranty coverage and parts availability are at risk. That undercuts the "easy battery replacement" requirement. [3] reports a CPSC warning about certain older Rad batteries.
- **Mechanical-brake bikes:** [7] says Ride1Up's Core-5 and Aventon's Soltera.2 have mechanical brakes. It advises looking elsewhere for hilly routes, and says the Core-5 would suit hydraulic brakes better.

**Uncertainty:** None of these sources tested a 10-mile hilly commute directly, and the battery-availability evidence is thin across the board.

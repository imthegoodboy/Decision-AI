**My pick: Fitbit Air ($99).** It has the best mix of sleep and heart-rate performance, price, and no mandatory subscription among the sources I was given.

**Why the Fitbit Air**
- It costs $99 and is subscription-free for its core features. Tom's Guide says it is "just as reliable at logging sleep trends and fitness efforts as Garmin watches that cost 2.5x as much" [2].
- It tracks heart rate continuously. It also has SpO2 and skin-temperature sensors, and it reports heart rate variability, breathing rate and a daily readiness score [2].
- Its sleep reports cover duration, time in each stage, restlessness and a score out of 100. Tom's Guide says they are on par with some of the best smartwatches of 2026 [2].
- Terra's data on thousands of users found Fitbit's sleep data "cleaner overall", with low outlier rates and good nap detection. Only Garmin's data was as consistent [6]. That measures consistency, not accuracy against a sleep lab.
- Google Health Premium is optional at $9.99 a month or $99 a year. It adds AI sleep-trend recommendations, and the tracker ships with a three-month trial [2].

**Caveats**
- Tom's Guide tested it for just over a week [2], and none of my sources benchmarks any tracker against clinical sleep or ECG equipment.
- It has no screen and no onboard GPS, so you need your phone for location tracking. Automatic workout detection only starts after 15 minutes [2].

**Alternatives**
- **Garmin vívosmart 5:** Popular Science's top budget pick. It offers accurate heart-rate and sleep tracking, and Garmin does not charge extra to access your data [7]. That review dates from 2023, and I have no current price. Terra found Garmin's sleep data the most consistent, with the lowest variability and fewest outliers [6]. Choose this if you want the strongest "everything is free" guarantee.
- **Amazfit Balance ($199):** Live Science's top overall pick. Its heart rate, breathing rate and HRV readings were accurate in testing, and its battery lasts up to two weeks. It is aimed at sleep, recovery and wellness. Some extras, such as the AI coach chatbot, are paywalled, but they are not essential [1].
- **Amazfit Helio Strap and Polar Loop:** Both are screenless and subscription-free, but I found no accuracy data for them. The Helio Strap costs the same as the Air, and the Polar Loop costs about twice as much [2].

**Avoid**
- **Whoop:** It requires a subscription [2][7].
- **Xiaomi Band 7 Pro:** Popular Science says its sleep tracking isn't accurate [7].
- **Fitbit Inspire 3:** Popular Science says detailed sleep data and other insights need Premium [7].

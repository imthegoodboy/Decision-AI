**Recommendation: a certified pre-owned Trek Allant+ 7 (Bosch mid-drive), with the Ride1Up Turris as the best new-bike alternative.**

**Why the Allant+ 7.** Hills are the hardest part of your commute, and a mid-drive motor handles them best. Upway's guide says mid-drives feel more natural on climbs and that the difference is noticeable on hilly rides [6]. The Allant+ 7 has a Bosch Performance Line CX motor with 85 Nm of torque, and its writer says the feel on a hilly commute is "substantial" [6]. Brakes are hydraulic discs as standard [6]. Battery replacement is your other concern. The Allant+ 7's 500Wh removable battery is UL certified and "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6]. That is the clearest battery-availability statement in the sources. Ten miles each way fits comfortably in a 500Wh pack. The guide also says it is a Class 1 bike with no throttle [6].

**Caveats.** The $1,500 price applies to used bikes only. Upway lists the Allant+ 7 and its peers under $1,500 against original prices of $2,000 to $3,500, with inspection, a one-year warranty and a 14-day return window [6]. Buying new at this budget would mean a hub motor instead. Upway is also the retailer behind this recommendation [6].

**Best new alternative: Ride1Up Turris ($1,295).** One roundup ranks it best overall under $1,500. It has a 750W hub motor that "climbs 10%+ grades," a 672Wh battery, and Tektro hydraulic brakes [4]. That source has no named author or publisher and contradicts itself in places, so treat it with some caution. For example, it lists the Lectric XP 3.0 brakes as mechanical in its table but hydraulic in its text [4]. Ride1Up is direct-to-consumer only, so you will not have a local dealer for service [7].

**Other options.**
- **Lectric XP4 ($999).** It has a torque sensor, a custom hydraulic brake system, a 500W motor and a 499Wh battery [1]. Its limits are weight (over 70 lbs) and small 20-inch folding-bike wheels [1].
- **Aventon Pace 500.3.** It has hydraulic brakes, but its 500W motor is described as slightly underpowered on real hills with a full bag [4]. The sources also disagree on its price ($1,399 versus $1,699) [4][8].
- **Avoid for hills:** mechanical-brake bikes such as the Aventon Soltera.2, which that guide says to skip for hilly routes [7].
- **Rad Power.** A CPSC warning covers certain older Rad batteries [3]. Rad has also been through bankruptcy, which puts parts and warranty support at risk [5].

**Before you buy.** Confirm the exact battery model number and ask the seller or manufacturer how a replacement is ordered. Prefer bikes with named cells (Samsung or LG) and UL 2271 or UL 2849 certification [7].

**Recommendation: Amazfit Balance (about $199, often discounted)**

None of the sources tested sleep accuracy against a lab standard such as polysomnography. I can't prove any device is "most accurate". The Amazfit Balance is the best-supported choice because it has the strongest heart-rate evidence and no meaningful subscription lock.

**Why the Amazfit Balance**
- Live Science's hands-on testing found its heart rate, breathing rate and HRV measurements accurate. It is priced under $200 and is often on sale [1].
- Steady State Reviews also calls its heart-rate tracking excellent and names it the strongest option at the $199 ceiling [3].
- Both sources say the paywalled extras are minor. The only one named is the AI coach chatbot, and neither says anything essential is locked [1][3].
- Live Science describes it as focused on sleep, recovery and wellness, with up to two weeks of battery [1].
- Its sleep accuracy rests on that general praise. I found no sleep-specific accuracy test, so my confidence on sleep is moderate.

**Where the sources disagree**
- **Xiaomi Band 7 Pro:** Steady State Reviews picks it for the most accurate budget sleep tracking [3]. Popular Science lists "sleep tracking isn't accurate" as a con [8]. The Popular Science review is older (2023), and Steady State's claim comes from a single reviewer, so I would not rely on it for sleep.
- **Garmin sleep reliability:** Steady State says Terra's benchmark showed Garmin with the worst variability and outlier rates [3]. Terra's report says the opposite: Garmin was the most consistent, with the lowest variability and very low outliers [7]. I trust Terra, the primary source, over the secondary summary. Terra also measures consistency, not accuracy against a gold standard, and its samples may not be comparable across brands [7].
- **Fitbit Air ($99):** Tom's Guide says it is subscription-free for core features and gives detailed sleep reports comparable to some top smartwatches [2]. Steady State says sleep stages and the nightly score require Premium ($9.99 a month) after the three-month trial ends [3]. Tom's Guide does confirm that some sleep features need Premium, such as AI-analyzed sleep trend recommendations [2]. It also mentions the three-month trial, so its tester may have seen features that later lock [2]. This is unresolved, so check before buying.

**Alternatives**
- **Garmin vivosmart 5:** Popular Science calls it accurate for heart rate and sleep, with free access to all your data [8]. Its price isn't given in the sources, so confirm it's under $200. It has no built-in GPS [8].
- **Fitbit Air:** This is the cheapest option and the lightest. It only suits you if you accept possible Premium costs for sleep detail, and it has no onboard GPS [2][3].
- **Avoid:** Whoop needs a yearly membership of $199 to $359 [3]. The Fitbit Inspire 3 puts detailed sleep data behind Premium [3][8].

I discounted two sources. One was a generic list with no testing detail and an internal inconsistency over Fitbit subscriptions [6]. The other was a 2020 Gizmodo article on older models [5]. A fourth link was a dead page [4].

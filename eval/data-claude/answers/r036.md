## Recommendation
Wealthfront, with Betterment a close second. The evidence-weighted ranking was Wealthfront 72, Betterment 68, Vanguard Digital Advisor 55.5 and Schwab 47. Confidence in Wealthfront is only moderate because its tax-loss harvesting (TLH) at $5,000 is disputed.

## Why
- Wealthfront charges a flat 0.25% with no monthly minimum fee, about $12.50 a year on $5,000 [7]. Several sources give the same 0.25% fee [2][3][5].
- The minimum is $500, so $5,000 clears it easily [2][3][5].
- Several sources credit Wealthfront with daily TLH [2]. One says it covers all accounts [4] and another says "advanced" TLH [5]. Only [2] is quoted on this, and it is a low-reliability, undated source.
- Betterment is costlier at this balance. It charges 0.25%, or $5/month on balances under $25,000 [2]. NerdWallet also lists "0.25% or $5/month" [1]. $5 a month is about $60 a year, roughly 1.2% of $5,000 [7].

## Trade-offs
- Wealthfront's TLH at $5,000 is not certain (see Conflicting evidence).
- Betterment has the clearest tax story for small accounts. It offers TLH on taxable accounts at every balance level, and TLH+ coordinates between IRA and taxable accounts [7]. Source [2] says the same. It has a $10 minimum [1].
- TLH only helps in taxable accounts. If the $5,000 goes into a Roth IRA, tax features matter much less [7].

## Alternatives
- **Betterment:** pick it if you want TLH confirmed at every balance, or you expect to deposit enough to avoid the flat fee. I could not verify the exact conditions for avoiding it.
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios:** it has no advisory fee, only underlying ETF expenses [7], and a $5,000 minimum [7]. It sends 6-10% of the portfolio to cash [2]. NerdWallet reports TLH only from $50,000 [1], which is a poor fit for your tax priority.
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor:** about 0.15% net with a $100 minimum, no TLH per [2], so it suits a Roth IRA. NerdWallet lists it among robo-advisors with automated TLH [1].

## Conflicting evidence
- **Schwab TLH:** [1] (the most reliable source, reliability 91 after checking) and [2] say it needs $50,000. [7] says any taxable account at $5,000 qualifies. I trust [1] and [2]. The tool marked [7]'s claim contradicted.
- **Schwab cash drag:** [2] says 6-10% and [4] says 6-30%. [7] says about 2%. The tool marked the 2% figure contradicted, so treat it as unreliable.
- **Wealthfront TLH:** [7] says no harvesting below $100,000, while [2], [4] and [5] imply it applies. [7] recommends Schwab, which its own claims undercut. The tool marked its Wealthfront claim contradicted. The sources that disagree with [7] are lower-tier, so this is unresolved.
- **Wealthfront direct indexing:** [6] says it unlocks at $5,000, but [6] is dated 2023. [2], [3] and [5] say $100,000 or more.
- **Vanguard TLH:** [2] and [7] say none, while [1] lists it as offering TLH.

## Before you decide
- No provider pages were retrieved, and most sources are secondary reviews. Confirm on Wealthfront's and Betterment's own sites that TLH applies at $5,000 and what fee you would pay.
- Decide first whether this money goes in a taxable account or a Roth IRA.
- Fees and thresholds change, so check current rates.

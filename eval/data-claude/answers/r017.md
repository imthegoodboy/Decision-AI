**Recommendation: take IELTS for UKVI (Academic), unless speed matters more to you than acceptance. In that case, take PTE Academic UKVI. Check which test your university names before you book either one.**

**Why IELTS for UKVI**
- Both IELTS for UKVI and PTE Academic UKVI are Home Office-approved Secure English Language Tests (SELTs) and are accepted for study in the UK [7][8].
- IELTS has the broader recognition. IDP, which co-owns IELTS and so has a commercial interest, says it is accepted by over 13,500 organisations against about 3,000 for PTE [7]. The Study Abroad Ledger gives the same figures [3]. These are global counts, not a count of UK universities.
- IELTS for UKVI uses the same papers as standard IELTS, so normal practice material works. It also offers paper-based testing and a face-to-face speaking test [3][7][8].

**Why PTE Academic UKVI**
- PTE is fully computer-based and AI-scored. One guide says results often arrive within 48 hours [1].

**Where the sources disagree**
- **Speed of UKVI results.** Pearson's own figure is 85.7% of candidates getting scores within 2 days, but it is from 2018 and covers standard PTE Academic, not the UKVI version [4]. The Ledger says UKVI results typically take 7 to 10 working days because of Home Office identity checks [3]. I found no source that confirms or contradicts this. Treat PTE UKVI's speed advantage as unproven, and expect more than 2 days.
- **Duolingo.** The Ledger says Duolingo English Test UKVI became an approved SELT in early 2026, and recommends it as the fastest option [3]. 7ESL, which was checked in September 2026 and links to GOV.UK, lists the approved tests without Duolingo. It says Duolingo is not a SELT and is only accepted where a university assesses English itself [8]. The Ledger's claim stands alone, so I would not rely on it. Check GOV.UK before considering Duolingo.
- **Result validity.** The Ledger says IELTS UKVI results are valid for only 12 months, against two years for other providers [3]. 7ESL says a SELT result must be from within the two years before you apply [8]. I can't tell which is right. Confirm on GOV.UK, and avoid testing very early either way.

**Other options**
- Other approved SELTs include LanguageCert International ESOL, Trinity ISE (taken inside the UK) and PSI Skills for English UKVI (taken outside the UK) [8]. They have narrower recognition.
- Universities may accept non-SELT tests such as TOEFL iBT or Cambridge C1 Advanced [8]. Sheffield, for example, accepts all IELTS qualifications for direct entry to its postgraduate courses [2]. Whether you need a SELT depends on your university, so start with the test named in your offer letter [3][8].

**Practical steps**
- Book the UKVI version at an approved centre [1][8].
- Check the score your university and visa route require (CEFR B2 for degree-level study) [8].
- Leave time for a resit.

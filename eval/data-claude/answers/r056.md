**Recommendation: a used Trek Allant+ 7 from Upway, with the Ride1Up Turris as the best new-bike option.**

**Why the Allant+ 7.** Two of your needs are hills and battery replacement. This is the only bike in the sources that speaks to both.
- It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive with 85 Nm of torque. The reviewer says "the difference in feel on a hilly commute is substantial" [6]. Upway's FAQ also says a mid-drive is worth it for hilly commutes because it works through the gears [6].
- Hydraulic disc brakes are standard [6].
- The 500Wh removable battery is UL certified and "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6]. No other bike in the sources has a battery availability claim this direct.

**Caveats on that pick.**
- The bike is only under $1,500 as a used or refurbished unit. It originally retailed for $2,000 to $3,500, and Upway gives it a one-year warranty and a 14-day return window [6].
- Upway is a reseller, so the praise comes from a seller. Stock and price are not guaranteed.
- A roundup elsewhere says mid-drives "don't show up at this price" [4]. That is true for new bikes, and it is why the Allant+ is only an option on the used market.

**Best new alternative: Ride1Up Turris ($1,295).**
- It has a 750W hub motor, which the review says climbs 10%+ grades, plus Tektro hydraulic brakes and a 672Wh battery [4].
- Its claimed 30–45 mile range is far more than a 10-mile commute needs [4].
- This is a single source with no named author or publisher, so treat the numbers cautiously.
- The source says nothing about replacement batteries, so check with Ride1Up before buying.

**Other options.**
- **Lectric XP4 ($999)** has a 500W motor, a 499Wh battery, a torque sensor and a custom hydraulic brake system [1]. It is a heavy folding bike (over 70 lbs) [1]. Its hill performance is not specifically tested in the sources, and none of them covers battery replacement.
- **Aventon Pace 500.3** has hydraulic brakes. One source warns its 500W motor feels underpowered on a real hill with a full bag [4]. I would skip it if your hills are serious.

**Where the sources disagree.**
- **Lectric XP 3.0 brakes.** [4] lists them as "Mechanical" in its table but "hydraulic" in its text. [7] says hydraulic, and it is a separate source. I trust hydraulic, and source [4]'s inconsistency lowers my confidence in its other details.
- **Pace 500.3 price.** [4] gives $1,399. A 2024 article gives $1,699 [8]. Prices change, so the newer figure is more plausible, but confirm before buying.

**What to avoid.**
- Rad Power is a risky choice for battery replacement. The CPSC warned about certain Rad batteries (specific model numbers) [3]. One source says the company went through bankruptcy and parts availability "could dry up" [5].
- Entry-level bikes with mechanical brakes. Sources warn against them on hilly routes [7].

**Bottom line.** The evidence is thin. Nearly every claim comes from a single source, several of them commercial or anonymous. Before buying, verify the exact battery part number and where to buy a replacement, and test the brakes on a steep descent.

**Recommendation: Fitbit Air ($99), with a caveat that no source tests accuracy rigorously.**

Tom's Guide says the Air costs $99 and is subscription-free for core features, with 24/7 heart-rate monitoring and detailed nightly sleep reports [2]. Its reviewer calls it as reliable at logging sleep trends as Garmin watches costing 2.5x as much [2]. In the review's side-by-side with a Garmin Forerunner 70, the two devices' average sleeping heart rates were 66 and 65 bpm [2]. That is close agreement, though it is one night of data.

The subscription is optional. Google Health Premium costs $9.99 a month or $99 a year and unlocks AI-backed insights, including sleep-trend recommendations [2]. The Air also ships with a three-month trial [2]. The raw tracking and sleep reports do not need it [2]. The trade-offs are that it has no screen, no onboard GPS or altimeter, and about a week of battery [2].

**Where the evidence is thin or conflicting**
- The same Tom's Guide comparison shows the Air and the Garmin disagreeing on sleep stages. Total sleep was 7h37 versus 8h09, and REM was 30 minutes versus 1h35 [2]. So "on par" most likely refers to overall trends rather than stage-level precision. The Air verdict also rests on one reviewer testing it for about a week [2].
- Terra's dataset of thousands of users found Garmin the most consistent sleep platform. Fitbit was also clean, with low outlier rates [6]. That measures data consistency, not accuracy against a clinical standard, and the user groups may differ [6]. I treat it as modest support. It does not show the Air itself is accurate.
- Fitbit's subscription story varies by source. For the Inspire 3, Popular Science says detailed sleep data sits behind Premium [7]. That is a 2023 article about a different device. A third list claims the Charge 8 is subscription-free while also listing paywalled features [5]. I would not weight [5] much, since it is a generic, unattributed list with no test data shown.

**Alternatives**
- **Amazfit Balance ($199):** Live Science says its heart rate sensors were "top-class" in its tests, and it named the Balance its best overall tracker. It also describes the watch as focused on sleep and recovery [1]. It has a screen, GPS and about two weeks of battery [1]. Some features, such as an AI coach, are paywalled but non-essential [1]. The source praises heart rate accuracy but gives no explicit sleep-accuracy result.
- **Garmin vívosmart 5:** Popular Science lists accurate heart rate and sleep tracking, and says Garmin does not charge extra for access to your data [7]. That article is from 2023 and the excerpt gives no price, so check that it is under $200 today.

Skip Whoop, which requires a pricey subscription [7]. Gizmodo's 2020 comparison is too dated to inform a current purchase [4]. One source link returned only an error page [3].

Bottom line: the Fitbit Air is the cheapest option with reasonable evidence on both sleep and heart rate. If you want a screen and GPS, the Amazfit Balance is the stronger all-rounder.

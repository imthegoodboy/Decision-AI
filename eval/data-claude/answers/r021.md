## Recommendation
Coursera, using a Python-based Professional Certificate (IBM Data Analyst or Meta Data Analyst). The evidence-weighted ranking put it first (66/100), ahead of Dataquest (61), DataCamp (53) and edX (50.5), with only moderate confidence.

## Why
- IBM's certificate "focuses on developing key technical skills, including the use of Python and SQL for data analysis" [1]. Meta's five-course certificate covers Python with a statistics emphasis [1]. Google's certificate teaches R, so avoid it if you want Python [1][7].
- The certificates carry brand recognition, though they are completion-based rather than proctored exams [4].
- Coursera offers guided projects for hands-on work [6], from $9.99 [2].
- Auditing for free gives no certificate [2]. Professional Certificates started around $49 a month in 2024 [2].

## Trade-offs
- Pace is the main risk. Coursera estimates IBM at 4 months at 10 hours a week [1]. At 5 hours a week, expect roughly double (my inference). A certification of 300 hours would take over a year at that pace [4].
- One working learner took about a month per IBM course [7]. That is a single anecdote, and the hands-on labs they described were in Excel, not Python [7].

## Alternatives
- Dataquest: a project-based Python-for-data-analysis path at 3-6 months at 5-10 hours a week [4]. Choose it if portfolio projects matter more than a certificate. Hiring managers reportedly value projects over credentials [4]. However, Dataquest wrote that source, and nothing independent confirms it.
- edX: you can pay to upgrade a free course to an official certificate [2]. One source says it has fewer practical industry certifications than Coursera [6]. The Python option the sources mention, Harvard CS50P, is introductory and takes roughly 100-150 hours [4].
- DataCamp: one reviewer calls its method more hands-on [2]. No source covers its certificate or curriculum, so I can't recommend it on this evidence.

## Conflicting evidence
- Course count: Coursera's 2025 page says the IBM certificate has 11 courses [1], while a 2024 learner says 9 [7]. I trust Coursera's newer page, since the learner may have seen an older version.
- Accreditation: one reviewer calls Coursera and edX certificates "accredited" [2]. Dataquest says they are completion-based, and notes Harvard calls its offerings "not accredited academic offerings" [4]. The latter is more precise, so treat these as recognised brand credentials, not formal qualifications.
- Sources [1] and [3] are mirrors of one Coursera page. [2] and [5] are mirrors of one Upskillwise review. They are not independent confirmation.

## Before you decide
- Most sources are from 2024-2025. Coursera wrote [1] and Dataquest wrote [4], and each sells courses.
- Check DataCamp's certificate directly if it interests you.
- Preview the first module for free, and confirm the current price and course count before paying.

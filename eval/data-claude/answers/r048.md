## Recommendation
Buy the **Eureka Forbes 355 (about ₹9,999)**. It is a close call with the Qubo Q600, explained below. Filter cost for both is unverified.

## Why
- **Enough CADR.** It is rated 355 m³/h with a True HEPA H13 filter [1][2]. A common rule says CADR should be at least two-thirds of the floor area in sq ft, about 235 for your room [1][2]. For Delhi, one source suggests cycling the room's air 3-4 times an hour, so 300 m³/h or more [2][3]. 355 clears both.
- **True HEPA H13, plus carbon.** Both sources list H13 with a pre-filter and carbon stage [1][2]. Guides say to avoid "HEPA-type" labels [1][2][3].
- **Price.** About ₹9,999, well under budget [1][2]. That leaves room for filters.

## Trade-offs
- **Noise.** It is rated 63 dB, which is loud for a bedroom [1][2]. The guides call under 30 dB the comfort zone [1][2]. Plan to use a low speed at night.
- **Plasma stage.** The spec sheet lists one, and ionising features can produce ozone [2]. Check whether it can be switched off.
- **Replacement filters.** I found no filter price for this model in any source, so your "affordable filters" priority is unverified. Typical annual replacement costs are ₹1,500-5,000 [1][2]. Large units with full filter sets of ₹8,000 or more should be avoided [2][3].

## Alternatives
- **Qubo Q600 (₹14,490).** It has 450 m³/h and H13 [1][2]. The evidence-weighted ranking from my tool put it first. I still chose the Eureka Forbes because it is about ₹4,500 cheaper and 355 m³/h already exceeds the target. The Q600 costs nearly your full budget and is described as built for large open-plan areas at about 55 dB [2]. It is also not clearly worse, so choose it if you want maximum power and have checked its filter price.
- **Realme TechLife (₹7,999).** It has 330 m³/h [2][7]. Its HEPA grade is disputed (see below).
- **Too small or not suitable.** The Qubo Q200 is 150 m³/h and rated for 200 sq ft [1][2][6]. The Philips AC1711 is 300 m³/h but about 50 dB [1][2]. The Levoit Core 300 is quiet but described as low on CADR [1][2].

## Conflicting evidence
- **Realme HEPA grade.** [2] says True HEPA H13. [7] is a hands-on review that says H12, removing 99.95% of particles. I trust [7] on the hardware, but it dates from 2021, so the model may have changed. [2] is newer but cites nothing. This is unresolved, and since you want true HEPA I would not rely on Realme.
- **Honeywell Air Touch V5 CADR.** [1] says 380 m³/h and [2] says 250 m³/h. Neither cites a test, so I would not rely on this model.
- **Mi Air Purifier 4 price.** [1] gives ₹14,999 on Flipkart and ₹17,999 on Amazon. [2] says ₹17,999 on both. It is likely over budget.
- **Source reliability.** [4] is an irrelevant homepage and [5] duplicates [3]. [1] says its picks are based on advertised specifications, with no hands-on testing.

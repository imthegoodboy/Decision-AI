## Recommendation

Get the **Amazfit Balance** ($199, often on sale) [1]. It has the best-supported heart-rate accuracy among sub-$200 options, and nothing essential is paywalled. Sleep accuracy is the weak point for every device here: none of the sources give reliable, independent sleep-accuracy data for a specific model. The tool's ranking has the Balance only narrowly ahead of the Garmin vivosmart 5 (69.5 vs 69).

## Why

- Live Science says the Balance's heart rate and body composition sensors were "top-class in our tests" [1]. It also says the watch accurately measured heart rate, breathing rate and HRV [1]. Steady State also calls its heart-rate tracking excellent [3].
- Only extras such as the AI coach chatbot sit behind a paywall, and "nothing essential does" [3]. Live Science agrees that these paid features are not essential [1].

## Trade-offs

- The AI coach and a few other extras cost extra [1][3].
- The display lacks Gorilla Glass, so it suits the gym and office better than rough outdoor use [1][3].
- The heart-rate evidence comes from reviewers' hands-on tests, not lab validation. Sleep accuracy for the Balance is unverified.

## Alternatives

- **Garmin vivosmart 5:** Garmin does not charge extra to access your data [8][3]. Its heart-rate sensor is described as solid [3], and Popular Science lists accurate heart-rate and sleep tracking as a pro [8]. That claim is a 2023 pros bullet with no test data, so treat it as weak. It has no built-in GPS [3][8].
- **Fitbit Air ($99):** It is subscription-free for core features [2], but see the conflict below.
- **Avoid if you want no subscription:** Whoop, with membership at $199 to $359 a year [3]. The Fitbit Inspire 3 also needs Premium for detailed sleep data [8][3].

## Conflicting evidence

- **Xiaomi Band 7 Pro sleep:** Steady State calls it the most accurate budget band tested [3]. Popular Science says "Sleep tracking isn't accurate" [8]. Neither shows data, and the tool marked both contradicted. I would not rely on either.
- **Fitbit Air sleep:** Tom's Guide says it gives detailed sleep reports on par with top smartwatches [2]. Steady State says sleep stages and the sleep score need Premium [3]. Tom's Guide is a hands-on review with a higher reliability score, but it also says some sleep-trend insights need Premium [2]. This is unresolved.
- **Garmin sleep:** Steady State says Terra's benchmark showed Garmin with the highest outlier rates [3]. Terra itself reports the opposite: Garmin was the most consistent, with the lowest variability and outliers [7]. Trust Terra, since Steady State misstates it. Terra measures consistency across users, not accuracy against a reference, and Apple stood out for low deep sleep and data errors [7].

## Before you decide

- Check the current price and the return policy.
- If sleep accuracy matters most, wear the device for a week next to a second tracker. Also look for independent validation studies, which none of these sources provide.

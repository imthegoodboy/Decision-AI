**Recommendation: TVS iQube (a larger-battery variant), with a test ride first. My confidence is moderate, because the sources are thin on long-term reliability.**

**Why the iQube.** Your priorities are reliability, real-world range and service, and the sources say little directly about the Ather, TVS or Bajaj service networks in Bengaluru. What they do show is that Ola is the weakest on service. Ola was reported to receive about 80,000 service complaints a month, with some owners waiting 30 to 45 days for repairs [4][6]. Bajaj and TVS held stable sales over the same period [4]. Ola's HyperService plan promised 1,000 centres by December 2024, a one-day fix guarantee and a backup scooter [8]. I found no source confirming that any of this was delivered. Autocar also noted Ola's suspension-failure reports and an Eco mode it called borderline dangerous in fast traffic [1].

For the iQube, listings give 32 litres of under-seat storage, strong build quality, a plush ride, a comfortable seat for two and a decent Eco mode [9]. Bigger-battery variants are described as giving decent daily range [9]. It costs about ₹95,124 to ₹1.30 lakh [9]. A 20 km commute is a light load for these scooters, though the sources agree real-world range runs well below certified figures [2][3]. None of the sources gives a measured real-world range for the iQube or Ather.

**Where the sources disagree.**
- **iQube top speed.** The Commute Ledger says the iQube runs out of breath near an indicated 60 km/h even in Power mode [2]. Autocar's test found Power mode reaches an indicated 80 km/h and holds it [1][7], and a spec listing gives 78 to 82 km/h [9]. I trust Autocar and the spec listing more. [2] repeats much of Autocar's Mumbai test almost word for word, yet changes the iQube and Ola Eco-mode findings without explanation.
- **Ola complaints.** [2] says roughly 8,000 complaints a month and calls the crisis overblown. Three separate reports say 80,000 [4][6][8], so I discount [2].
- **Autocar's age.** Autocar's test was 2022 in Mumbai, so it is dated for Bengaluru [1].

**Alternatives.**
- **Bajaj Chetak:** the comfort pick. It has a wide, well-contoured seat, a firm but absorbent ride, an indicated top speed of 69 km/h that feels stretched on faster roads, and a shallow boot [1]. It suits mostly inner-city routes. Its price is about ₹1.24 to 1.52 lakh [9].
- **Ather 450X:** the best handling and throttle response, and usable Eco mode at low charge. It is the least comfortable, with a cramped fit for riders over about 5ft 10in and a harsh ride on broken roads [1]. It costs about ₹1.51 to 1.90 lakh [9]. Pick it if you value ride feel over comfort.
- **Ola S1 Pro:** the fastest, with a 36-litre boot [1]. Its service record rules it out for me given your priorities. [2] recommends it, but I trust that source least.
- **Ampere (Greaves):** I would treat it as unverified. The only source is Ampere's own marketing, which claims 420+ service points across India and real-range figures [3].

**Before buying.** Ask each dealer how many service centres are near your home and office, and check recent owner reviews on service. Test-ride on your actual route, including a flyover and an arterial road like the Outer Ring Road. If you regularly ride above about 65 km/h, the iQube's Power-mode top speed is worth confirming yourself, given the disagreement above.

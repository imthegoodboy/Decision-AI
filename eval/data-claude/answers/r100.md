**Recommendation: Trek Allant+ 7 (certified pre-owned, under $1,500)**

For a hilly 10-mile commute, I'd pick the Trek Allant+ 7. It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive motor with 85 Nm of torque, and the source says it "pulls cleanly" on hills [6]. Mid-drives feel more natural on climbs, and one source says that is worth it for hilly commutes [6]. Almost every new bike under $1,500 uses a hub motor instead [7].

- **Brakes:** Hydraulic disc brakes are standard [6]. They give more stopping power with less hand effort and self-adjust as pads wear [7].
- **Battery replacement:** This is the strongest match to your requirement. The 500Wh removable battery is UL certified and "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6]. No other source I read makes that claim for a specific bike.
- **Price:** The Allant+ 7 is a bike that originally retailed for $2,000 to $3,500. Upway sells it for under $1,500, inspected and certified, with a one-year warranty and a 14-day return window [6].

Trade-offs: you're buying used, and the one-year warranty is short. At 500Wh, range is fine for 20 round-trip miles, but check battery health on the specific unit.

**Best new alternative: Ride1Up Turris ($1,295)**
One roundup rates it best overall under $1,500. It has a 750W hub motor that climbs 10%+ grades, Tektro hydraulic brakes, and a 672Wh battery with 35–40 miles of real-world range [4]. That source also says to pick the Turris over the Pace 500.3 if your commute involves significant climbing [4]. Its publisher is unidentified, and I found nothing on Turris battery replacement, so ask Ride1Up about spare packs before buying. Ride1Up is also direct-to-consumer only, so there is no local dealer [7].

**Other options**
- **Aventon Pace 500.3:** It has hydraulic brakes and a semi-integrated battery, and is rated near-perfect for flat urban terrain [4]. Its 500W motor feels underpowered on real hills with a loaded bag [4]. Aventon also has a large bike-shop footprint for service [3]. Prices differ by source ($1,399 in [4], $1,699 in an older article [8]), so verify the current price.
- **Lectric XP4 ($999):** It has hydraulic brakes, a torque sensor, a 500W motor and a 499Wh battery [1]. It's a heavy folding bike at over 70 lbs [1]. Reviewers found its power and brakes strong for the price [1].
- **Cannondale Adventure Neo 4:** It has a Bosch mid-drive and hydraulic brakes, but only 40 Nm of torque and a 400Wh removable battery [6]. It's better for city hills than for steep climbs.

**What to avoid**
- **Rad Power:** The CPSC warned in November 2025 about certain Rad batteries [3], and one source flags the company's bankruptcy as a risk to parts and warranty [5]. That cuts against your battery-availability requirement.
- **Mechanical-brake bikes:** The Soltera.2 and Ride1Up Core-5 have mechanical brakes. One source calls the Soltera.2 a poor fit for hilly routes and suggests budgeting for a brake upgrade on the Core-5 in hills [7].

**Bottom line:** Choose the Allant+ 7 if you're comfortable buying certified used. Choose the Turris if you want a new bike with a bigger hub motor.

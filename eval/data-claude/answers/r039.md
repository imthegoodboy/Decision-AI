## Recommendation

Get the **Fitbit Air ($99)**. It ranked first in the evidence-weighted scoring, but the lead over the Amazfit Balance is small (70 vs 68.5 out of 100). Confidence is moderate, because none of the sources benchmarks sleep or heart-rate accuracy against clinical references.

## Why

- It is subscription-free for core features. Tom's Guide describes it as $99 and subscription-free for core features [2].
- Its sleep reports are rated highly for the price. The same reviewer says they are on par with some of the best smartwatch models in 2026, covering sleep duration, sleep stages, restlessness and a nightly score [2]. This is a reviewer's comparison, not a lab accuracy test.
- Google Health Premium is optional, at $9.99 a month or $99 a year. It adds AI-backed insights [2].
- It is the cheapest option here and leaves plenty of room under the $200 limit [2].
- Sleep-data quality for Fitbit as a platform looks decent. Terra's analysis of user data found Fitbit "cleaner overall" and stable [6]. That study measures consistency, not accuracy. It covers Fitbit as a brand rather than the Air specifically, and the user groups on each platform may differ [6].

## Trade-offs

- The Air has no onboard GPS or altimeter, so you need to carry your phone for location tracking [2].
- I found no source that explicitly tests the Air's heart-rate accuracy. It does track heart rate continuously [2], but accuracy is not verified.

## Alternatives

- **Amazfit Balance ($199, often on sale):** Live Science's testers found its heart-rate sensors top-class [1]. Choose it if you want a smartwatch with tested heart-rate accuracy. Only non-essential extras, such as the AI coach, are paywalled [1]. I found no verified sleep-accuracy evidence for it.
- **Garmin vivosmart 5:** Popular Science lists accurate heart-rate and sleep tracking [7], and says Garmin does not charge extra to access your data [7]. That is a single 2023 article, and no price appears in the retrieved text. Terra also found Garmin's sleep data the most consistent of the four platforms it compared [6].
- **Avoid for sleep:** The Fitbit Inspire 3 puts detailed sleep data behind a Premium subscription [7]. Popular Science also says the Xiaomi Band 7 Pro's sleep tracking isn't accurate [7].

## Conflicting evidence

The sources do not directly contradict each other on the main picks. Some differences are worth noting.

- Source [5] lists the Fitbit Charge 8 as "subscription-free" but also says some advanced features are paywalled behind Fitbit Premium. That fits [2] and [7], where Fitbit's paid layer sits on top of free core tracking.
- Source [5] praises the Garmin Venu 4 and Amazfit Bip 6 Pro. It is a thin source and gives no testing detail or prices, so I did not rely on it.
- Source [3] was a 404 error page.
- Sources [1], [4] and [7] are older than a year, so their product details may be out of date.

## Before you decide

- Check current prices, because Amazfit and Fitbit are often discounted.
- Check which features count as "core" on the exact model you buy.
- If heart-rate accuracy matters most, compare the Air's readings against a chest strap during your return window. The Balance has the stronger tested evidence on that point.

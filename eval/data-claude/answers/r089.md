**Recommendation: Wealthfront, if the money goes in a taxable account.** Betterment is a close second.

**Why Wealthfront.** It charges a flat 0.25% a year with a $500 minimum [2]. On $5,000 that is about $12.50 a year, which is my arithmetic. It harvests tax losses daily and uses a large ETF universe to avoid wash sales [2]. Tax-loss harvesting only helps in taxable accounts, not retirement accounts [2]. NerdWallet confirms Wealthfront is one of the few robo-advisors with direct indexing at lower minimums, though that snippet was cut off before giving details [1].

**Where the sources disagree on Wealthfront.**
- The minimum for direct indexing is the biggest conflict. One source says it unlocks at $5,000, citing Wealthfront's Form ADV [6]. Others say $100,000 [2][4][5], and one says $500,000 [3]. Elsewhere the same source [6] also lists $100,000 for advanced features.
- I trust the Form ADV-based claim most because it cites a regulatory filing, but I can't confirm it. Treat direct indexing as possibly available at $5,000, and check Wealthfront's current brochure.
- One source says Wealthfront's tax-loss harvesting covers all accounts [4]. Another says its tax benefits mostly matter at higher balances [3]. Both are lower-quality sources, so expect modest tax savings at $5,000.

**Alternative: Betterment.** It is also 0.25% with no real minimum and offers tax-loss harvesting at all balance levels [2][1]. The catch is a flat $5 a month (some sources say $4) at low balances, which one source calls roughly 6% a year on $1,000 [2][1][8]. On $5,000 that would be about 1.2% a year (my arithmetic). Sources differ on the balance threshold ($20,000 to $25,000) and on whether deposits waive the fee [2][7]. If you can use the 0.25% tier, Betterment is strong, especially for goal-based saving [2]. Verify which fee applies to you before signing up.

**Schwab Intelligent Portfolios.** Your $5,000 just meets the minimum [2][5], and there is no management fee. However, 6–10% of the portfolio is held in cash [2], and another source says up to 30% [4]. Tax-loss harvesting requires $50,000 [1][2], so it fails your tax criterion. One source lists Schwab at 0.28% [3], which contradicts the others. I side with the majority, which describe the cost as cash drag.

**Other options.**
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor:** about 0.15% with a $100 minimum [2][1]. Sources conflict on tax-loss harvesting. One says it has none [2], while NerdWallet lists it among robo-advisors with automated harvesting [1]. NerdWallet is more credible, but verify before relying on it.
- **Fidelity Go:** charges no fee under $10,000 [5], but harvesting needs $25,000 [1].

**Caveats.** Several sources are blogs of unknown authorship, some carrying promotional links. Fees and minimums change, so confirm them on each provider's site. Tax-loss harvesting at $5,000 yields small savings, so a low fee matters more than advanced tax features right now.

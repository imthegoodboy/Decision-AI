**Recommendation: TVS iQube, tentatively. Test-ride it alongside the Ather 450S/450X before you pay.**

**Why the iQube.** Autocar India's 1,000 km Mumbai test calls the iQube and Chetak comfortable, easy-to-ride city commuters, with the TVS "a step above the Bajaj". It says the iQube gets to 40 kph quickly without overwhelming the rider, and has the best seat for two-up riding [1]. A 91Wheels listing, whose columns I matched by order, gives the iQube strong build quality, a plush ride and an Eco mode that doesn't feel sluggish. It also says the bigger-battery variants offer decent daily range [8]. The claimed range of the larger iQube is 140 km [4]. That is far more than a 20 km commute needs, even after the real-world haircut that [2] says applies to claimed figures. One drawback is that the portable charger takes up under-seat storage [8].

**Alternatives.**
- **Ather 450X/450S:** This is the best-handling scooter in the Autocar test, and its Eco mode stays usable below 10% charge [1]. The same review found the stiff ride harsh on poor roads and the seating cramped for riders over about 5ft 10in [1]. Bengaluru's roads make that a real concern. 91Wheels lists "good real-world range" as a 450S pro [8].
- **Bajaj Chetak:** It has a firm but absorbent ride and good ergonomics [1]. It is the slowest of the four to 40 kph, though only slightly behind the TVS, and has a 69 kph indicated top speed [1]. It is the heaviest at 132 kg, and Rushlane calls it dated in features [4]. These points don't conflict with Autocar, because they cover different things: Rushlane judged features and Autocar judged ride and ergonomics.
- **Ola S1:** I would avoid it for now, given your priority on service. Reports from September 2024 describe about 80,000 service complaints a month against 430 centres [3][5]. They also describe customers waiting 30 to 45 days for repairs [3]. Ola then announced HyperService, with a pledge of 1,000 centres by December 2024 and one-day fixes [7]. Those are promises, and no source shows they were delivered. Autocar also found that the S1 Pro cuts power when you brake and that its Eco mode is unsafe in traffic [1].
- **Ampere (Magnus Neo and others):** Ampere's own guide claims 420+ service points and 85 km real-world range for the Magnus Neo [2]. That source is the manufacturer's marketing, so I give it little weight.

**Where the evidence is thin.**
- No source gives Bengaluru service-centre counts or reliability data for Ather, TVS or Bajaj. I therefore can't rank them on your top two priorities.
- No source gives independently measured real-world range in Bengaluru for any of them.
- Autocar's test used 2022 models in Mumbai. The Ola service reports are from 2024, so conditions may have changed.
- The 91Wheels comparison page lists specs and pros and cons without labelling which column belongs to which scooter. I inferred the iQube column from the order of the listing.

Before buying, check the number of authorised service centres near your home and office, and read recent owner reviews for the exact variant.

**Recommendation: Fitbit Air ($99), with the Garmin vívosmart 5 as a close alternative**

No source here checks any tracker against a lab standard such as a sleep study or an ECG chest strap. Every accuracy claim below is a reviewer's judgement or a consistency measure, so treat "most accurate" as unproven.

**Why the Fitbit Air**
- It costs $99 and is subscription-free for core features. That covers continuous heart rate, nightly sleep reports and heart rate variability trends [2].
- The reviewer says its sleep reports are on par with some of the best smartwatches and as reliable as Garmin watches costing 2.5x as much [2]. That is one reviewer after about a week of testing [2].
- A side-by-side table in the same review shows large differences from a Garmin Forerunner 70, such as REM sleep of 30 minutes against 1 hour 35 minutes [2]. So "on par" is a loose claim.
- The paid Google Health Premium tier ($9.99 a month) adds AI insights and sleep trend recommendations. A three-month trial is included [2].
- It has no GPS, and automatic workout tracking only starts after 15 minutes [2].

**Alternatives**
- **Garmin vívosmart 5:** Popular Science gives it the top spot, citing accurate heart rate and sleep tracking and free access to all your data [7]. Terra's analysis of thousands of users found Garmin's sleep data the most consistent, with the lowest outlier rates [6]. That study measures consistency, not accuracy. It also compares platforms rather than specific devices, and the user groups may differ [6]. Popular Science's review dates from 2023, and none of the sources gives the vívosmart 5's price, so I could not confirm it is under $200 [7].
- **Amazfit Balance ($199):** Live Science found its heart rate and heart rate variability sensors "top-class" and it has a long battery life. It leans toward sleep and recovery, though the review gives no specific sleep-accuracy finding. Some features, such as the AI coach, sit behind a paywall [1]. It is the most expensive option and sits right at your limit [1].

**Where sources disagree or are weak**
- Subscription status for Fitbit is inconsistent. Popular Science says the older Inspire 3 needs Premium for detailed sleep data [7]. Tom's Guide says the newer Air's core sleep reports are free [2]. These are different products and dates, so I trust the more recent, product-specific Tom's Guide review for the Air [2]. Check which sleep details are paywalled before you buy.
- The V3tt3d list names the Garmin Venu 4 and Fitbit Charge 8 as subscription-free [5]. It gives no testing data or prices and has no named author. It also says some Charge 8 features are paywalled [5]. I gave it little weight.
- Gizmodo's 2020 comparison is too old to rely on. It does note that the Timex Metropolitan R's sleep tracking was better at big-picture metrics than at granular detail [4].
- Popular Science says the Xiaomi Band 7 Pro's sleep tracking is not accurate, so I would avoid it for this purpose [7].

**Bottom line:** If you want the lowest price and recent hardware, buy the Fitbit Air and skip Premium. If you want the strictest guarantee that your data is never paywalled, and Garmin's more consistent sleep data matters most, check the vívosmart 5's current price first. Both choices rest on thin evidence.

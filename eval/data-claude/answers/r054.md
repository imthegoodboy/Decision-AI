## Recommendation
Tentatively the **TVS iQube**, ideally one of the bigger-battery variants. The evidence is thin, and it separates the options on only one of your three priorities, so please treat this as a shortlist leader and not a firm verdict. The tool's evidence-weighted ranking puts the Ather 450X marginally first (53.5 vs 53 out of 100), but only 35% of its evidence is covered, because no source says anything about Ather's service or reliability. I chose the iQube because more of its evidence is verified (70%), and because it has no documented service problems.

## Why
- The clearest finding is negative, and it concerns Ola. Ola reportedly received about 80,000 service complaints a month in 2024 [4][6][8]. Many customers reported waiting 30 to 45 days for repairs [4]. Service and reliability are your top priorities, so this counts heavily against the S1.
- Ola's fix, HyperService, was a plan to reach 1,000 service centres by December 2024 [8]. The sources do not show whether it was delivered.
- In Autocar's ride test, the iQube's Power mode reached an indicated 80 kph and held those speeds [7]. The iQube also has good ergonomics, and its seat is the best of the four for riding two-up [7].
- One listing says the bigger-battery iQube variants offer decent range for daily use [9]. It gives no figures. A 20 km commute is a light load for any of these scooters [2].
- Bajaj and TVS kept stable electric sales while Ola's service troubles hit [4][6]. That is only an indirect sign of service quality.

## Trade-offs
- The iQube's regenerative braking is strong enough that it feels unnatural when you roll off the throttle [7].
- No source gives Bengaluru service-centre counts or reliability data for TVS, Ather or Bajaj. My pick rests on ruling out Ola and on comfort and speed, not on proof that TVS service is good.
- Real-world range in Bengaluru traffic runs well below the certified figures [2][3]. No source measures it for any named scooter.

## Alternatives
- **Ather 450X:** The best handling, and it tops out at a GPS-rated 80 kph [7]. Its Eco mode stays usable below 10% battery [7]. It is the least comfortable of the four, and cramped for riders over 5 ft 10 in [7].
- **Bajaj Chetak:** Excellent ergonomics [7]. Its indicated 69 kph top speed feels compromised on faster roads [7], so it suits mostly inner-city routes.
- **Ola S1:** Fast, and the largest boot. Autocar notes that it cuts power the moment you touch the brakes [7]. It also mentions a few suspension failures shared online [7]. I would avoid it given your priorities.

## Conflicting evidence
- **iQube top speed:** Autocar's direct test says Power mode reaches an indicated 80 kph [7]. The Commute Ledger says it runs out of breath near 60 kph [2]. Source [2] closely paraphrases the Autocar review but changes details, and it gives no test method. I trust Autocar [7] more, though that test was in Mumbai in 2022.
- **Ola complaints:** Source [2] says roughly 8,000 a month. Three other sources say 80,000 [4][6][8]. I trust the three, which report the same figure independently.
- **Ola Eco mode:** Autocar calls it borderline dangerous in fast traffic [7]. Source [2] says it clears flyovers fine. I side with Autocar, for the same reason as above.

## Before you decide
- Most of the performance evidence comes from 2022 Mumbai tests on older models [1][7]. Ask whether the current models behave the same way.
- Test-ride the iQube and the Ather on your own route.
- Check how close your nearest TVS, Ather and Bajaj service centres are, and read recent owner reviews for Bengaluru.
- Confirm on-road prices at a showroom. Listings show the iQube from ₹95,124 [9] and the 450X at ₹1.51 to 1.90 lakh [9].

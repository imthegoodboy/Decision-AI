**Recommendation: it depends on which of your two priorities matters more. For fastest results, take PTE Academic UKVI. For widest acceptance, take IELTS for UKVI Academic. If you must pick one, I'd pick IELTS for UKVI Academic, but only if the speed gap won't hurt your timeline.**

**Why PTE Academic UKVI wins on speed**

PTE Academic UKVI is fully computer-based and AI-scored. Results often arrive within 48 hours [1]. Pearson reported that 85.7% of test takers received scores within 2 days in 2018 [3]. Some results take longer, for example when they are flagged for extra review or a security check. If you have not heard after 5 days, Pearson asks you to contact them [3]. PTE Academic UKVI is approved for student visas [1], so it is a legitimate route for a master's applicant.

**Why IELTS for UKVI wins on acceptance**

IDP, which co-owns IELTS and so has a commercial interest, says IELTS is accepted by over 13,500 organisations. It says only around 3,000 accept PTE, and that IELTS is still favoured by most [6]. IELTS for UKVI Academic is also suitable for student visas [1]. It can be taken on paper or on computer, while PTE is computer-only [6]. That matters if you want to avoid typing your answers.

**Check these before you book**

- You need the UKVI version, taken at an approved test centre. A normal IELTS cannot be used where a Secure English Language Test (SELT) is required [7]. The content and format are the same as the standard tests, so ordinary practice material works [7].
- A SELT result must be from the two years before you apply [7].
- For degree-level study you need CEFR B2, and a SELT is one way to show it. A university that is a Higher Education Provider may also assess your English itself, so it can accept tests that are not SELTs, such as TOEFL iBT, Duolingo or Cambridge C1 Advanced [7]. Always check your university's list [7].
- Sheffield, for example, says universities may accept non-SELT qualifications, including all IELTS qualifications, for direct entry [2]. Your own university may differ.

**Alternatives**

- **LanguageCert International ESOL SELT:** this is on the approved SELT list [2][7], although my sources say little about how widely universities accept it.
- **Other approved SELTs:** the approved list also includes Trinity ISE and GESE (in the UK only) and PSI Skills for English UKVI (outside the UK) [7]. I would not start with these.
- **TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test, Cambridge C1 Advanced:** these are not SELTs, so they work only if your university assesses English itself [7].

**Bottom line**

1. Check your offer letter and your university's English requirements page first. Book whichever UKVI test it names [7].
2. If both are accepted and time is tight, take PTE Academic UKVI for the 48-hour turnaround [1].
3. If you want the safest, most widely recognised option and can wait longer, take IELTS for UKVI Academic [6].

My sources do not give Indian-specific test dates or centre availability, so check how soon you can actually get a seat at an approved centre in India.

**My pick: Amazfit Balance (about $199, often discounted).**

Live Science named it their best fitness tracker overall, reporting that its heart rate, breathing rate and heart rate variability readings were accurate in testing [1]. It is built around sleep, recovery and wellness, and it has up to two weeks of battery life, so you can wear it overnight without charging it [1]. A second review also calls its heart-rate tracking excellent and says nothing essential sits behind a paywall [3].

There are two caveats. First, the free-versus-paid line is not perfectly clean. Some extras, such as the AI fitness coach chatbot, are paywalled, though both reviews describe these as non-essential [1][3]. Second, I found no head-to-head sleep-accuracy measurement for the Balance in these sources. The sleep case rests on its design focus and reviewer praise, not on lab data. The display also lacks Gorilla Glass, so it is not ideal for rough outdoor use [1].

**Alternatives**

- **Garmin vivosmart 5 (best if you want a slim band with free data).** Popular Science says Garmin does not charge extra to access your data, and it lists accurate heart-rate and sleep tracking as a pro [8]. It has no built-in GPS and is an older review from 2023 [8]. Terra's large dataset found Garmin's sleep data the most consistent, with the lowest variability and very low outlier rates [7]. Note that Terra measures consistency, not accuracy against a lab standard.
- **Fitbit Air ($99).** Sources disagree here. Tom's Guide praises its sleep reports and calls it subscription-free for core features, while noting that AI-backed sleep trend recommendations need Premium at $9.99 a month [2]. Steady State Reviews says sleep stages and the nightly score are locked behind Premium [3]. Because of that conflict, check what the free tier shows before buying. The older Fitbit Inspire 3 clearly needs Premium for detailed sleep data [8].
- **Xiaomi Band 7 Pro.** Reviews contradict each other. One calls its sleep tracking the most accurate of any budget band [3], while Popular Science says its sleep tracking is not accurate [8]. I would skip it if sleep is your priority.

**Avoid if you want no subscription:** Whoop requires a membership costing $199 to $359 per year, according to Tom's Guide and Steady State Reviews [2][3]. Apple Watch sleep staging also looked the least reliable in Terra's data, with only about 10.5 percent deep sleep against nearly 18 percent on other platforms [7].

**Bottom line:** Buy the Amazfit Balance for the best mix of verified heart-rate accuracy, sleep focus and no required fee. Choose the Garmin vivosmart 5 if you prefer a slim band and fully free data. One source ranks the Garmin Venu 4 as best subscription-free overall, but that source gives no price and does not say it fits under $200 [6]. Treat the "most accurate" label cautiously, because these sources are mostly editorial reviews, not controlled accuracy tests.

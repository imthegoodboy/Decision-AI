**Recommendation: Wealthfront, in a taxable account, for a $5,000 start.**

**Why Wealthfront**
- It charges a flat 0.25% a year with a $500 minimum, so $5,000 clears the bar easily [2][3][5]. At that rate, $5,000 costs about $12.50 a year.
- Its tax-loss harvesting is described as daily, with replacement ETFs chosen to avoid wash sales [2]. Harvesting only matters in taxable accounts, not IRAs [2].
- Direct indexing is where the sources disagree. Several put it at $100,000 or more [2][4][5], and one says $500,000 [3]. One says it unlocks at $5,000 per Wealthfront's SEC filing [6]. NerdWallet only says Wealthfront offers it at "much lower minimums" than the usual six figures [1]. The filing-based claim is the most checkable, but I could not confirm it. Don't pick Wealthfront for direct indexing until you have checked Wealthfront's own site.

**Main alternative: Betterment.**
- It has no real minimum and charges 0.25% [1][2][3]. Source 2 says tax-loss harvesting applies to taxable accounts at all balances [2]. NerdWallet lists Betterment among robo-advisors offering automated harvesting from a $10 minimum [1].
- The fee structure is disputed. Some sources say you pay 0.25% [3]. Others say $5 a month, or 0.25% [1][8], or a flat $4–5 a month for balances under roughly $20,000–$25,000 [2][7]. On $5,000, $5 a month is $60 a year, or 1.2%. That would be far more expensive than Wealthfront. NerdWallet's "or" wording suggests the flat fee is a choice, but this is unresolved, so check Betterment's current terms before choosing it. I trust NerdWallet more than the anonymous or promotional pages, but it is brief on this point.

**Schwab Intelligent Portfolios is a poor fit at $5,000.**
- It has no management fee but a $5,000 minimum [2][4][5].
- It holds a large cash allocation, 6–10% in one source and up to 30% in another [2][4]. Source 2 calls that cash drag the effective cost.
- Automated tax-loss harvesting requires $50,000 [1][2]. Source 3's 0.28% fee for Schwab contradicts every other source, so I discount it.

**Others to consider**
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor:** about 0.15% with a $100 minimum [1][2]. The sources conflict on tax-loss harvesting. NerdWallet lists it as offering harvesting [1], and source 2 says it doesn't [2]. I'd lean toward NerdWallet but am unsure. Verify before relying on it.
- **Fidelity Go:** one source says $0 fees under $10,000 [5], but another says 0.35% [3]. NerdWallet says harvesting needs $25,000 [1].
- **Acorns:** monthly fees of about $3–12 are heavy on a small balance, and it has no tax-loss harvesting [3][5][8].

**Caveats**
- Most of these sources are affiliate or blog pages, and fees and minimums change. Confirm current terms on each provider's site.
- At $5,000, harvesting gains are likely small, since benefits are said to matter more at higher balances [3]. Low fees matter most, which favors Wealthfront, or Betterment if its 0.25% option is confirmed.

**Recommendation: Marcus by Goldman Sachs High-Yield Savings, with EverBank as the rate-focused alternative. Check current rates before opening either.**

**Why Marcus.** CBS News describes Marcus as having no monthly fee, no minimum opening deposit, no minimum balance to earn the full APY, and no cap on withdrawals or transfers. Its listed rate was 3.50% [2]. That fits your priorities of no fees and easy transfers, and the rate isn't conditional on anything. BankBonus says Marcus allows same-day transfers up to $100,000 and lets you link external accounts [4]. That comes from a single, older source (published 2023), so confirm it with the bank. Marcus has no checking account or ATM access [2], so you would move money to your regular bank when needed. For an emergency fund that is usually acceptable.

**FDIC caveat.** CBS says high-yield accounts are federally insured and lists "FDIC/NCUA insured: Yes" as a general takeaway [2]. None of my sources states Marcus's FDIC status explicitly, so confirm it on the bank's site before funding.

**Alternatives**
- **EverBank:** Fortune cites 3.90% APY, no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees, no required monthly behavior, and a 4.7-star app [6].
- **Vio Bank:** Fortune cites 4.00% on all balances with no fees and a $100 opening deposit. It has no checking account, so you must transfer to an outside bank before spending [6].
- **Axos ONE, LendingClub and SoFi:** Their top rates (about 4.00% to 4.21%) depend on conditions. Axos requires direct deposit and balance minimums and drops to 1.00% otherwise. LendingClub requires $250 or more in monthly deposits and otherwise pays 3.00%. SoFi's 4.00% includes a six-month boost for new members and falls to 1.00% without direct deposit or qualifying deposits [2][6]. A set-and-forget emergency fund suits these poorly.
- **Synchrony:** BankBonus says it has no fees or minimums and an optional ATM card [4]. CBS listed 3.40% [2].

**Where sources disagree.** The sources conflict on Axos. CBS lists 4.21% [2]. Fortune's body says up to 5.00%, while its own key takeaways say the top APY is 4.21% [6]. I would trust the 4.21% figure, since two statements support it. Both are conditional, so neither is a reliable headline rate for you. Fortune also claims Axos has $2.5 million in expanded FDIC coverage [6]. That appears in only one source, and it doesn't matter for $15,000.

**Sources I discounted.** The Gerald pages [1][3] describe a cash-advance app, not a savings account. The Ally review is from 2017 and has no current rates [5]. It does show a $10 fee after six monthly withdrawals, which may be outdated.

**Uncertainty.** Savings rates are variable and can change at any time [2]. The CBS data is from May 2026 and Fortune's is from July 2026, so today's rates may differ. Compare current APYs before you open an account.

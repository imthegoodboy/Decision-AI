## Recommendation
Open a **Marcus by Goldman Sachs** high-yield savings account. It ranked first in the evidence-weighted analysis (76.5/100). It is the cleanest fit for "no monthly fees, easy transfers," though it does not have the highest rate.

## Why
- CBS News says Marcus has no monthly fee, no minimum opening deposit, no minimum balance to earn the full APY, and no cap on withdrawals or transfers [2]. A second source also describes it as having no fees and no minimum deposit [4].
- The rate is unconditional. CBS lists 3.50% APY and says it "requires nothing beyond simply opening and funding the account" [2]. Several competitors only pay their top rate if you meet conditions (see Alternatives).
- For transfers, an older page says Marcus allows linking external accounts and same-day transfers up to $100,000 [4]. That page is undated and may be outdated, so treat this as partly verified.
- Savings accounts of this type are described as federally insured, and the FDIC national average is about 0.38%, so a high-yield account earns far more [2][6].

## Trade-offs
- At 3.50% [2], Marcus pays less than Vio Bank's 4.00% [6]. On $15,000 that gap is roughly $75 a year, by my arithmetic from the quoted rates.
- Marcus has no checking account and no ATM access [2]. You will need a separate checking account to spend the money.
- All savings rates are variable and can fall at any time [2].

## Alternatives
- **Vio Bank:** 4.00% on all balances with no monthly fees and no activity requirements [6]. It has no checking account or debit card, so you must transfer to an external bank before you can spend the money [6]. This is less convenient than Marcus.
- **EverBank:** 3.90% with no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees, and no required monthly behavior [6]. That source did not cover transfer details.
- **Axos Bank:** The top rate requires direct deposit and balance minimums, and it drops to a 1.00% base rate otherwise [2][6]. It is not ideal for a simple emergency fund.
- **Varo and SoFi:** Varo's 3.75% applies only to balances up to $5,000 [6], so it is unsuitable for $15,000. SoFi earns 1.00% without direct deposit [2].

## Conflicting evidence
The sources disagree on Axos's top rate. CBS says 4.21% [2]. Fortune's table says up to 5.00%, although its own key takeaways say the best rate is 4.21% [6]. I trust CBS more. The tool rated it 89/100 after verification, against 66/100 for Fortune, and Fortune contradicts itself. Both agree the rate depends on meeting deposit conditions. The Axos 5.00% claim was marked contradicted and I did not rely on it.

## Before you decide
- **FDIC status:** None of the sources explicitly state that Marcus or Vio Bank is FDIC insured. Only a generic statement about these accounts is supported [2][6]. Confirm each bank's FDIC coverage on its own site or at the FDIC's BankFind tool. Coverage is normally $250,000 per depositor, so $15,000 is well within it. That figure comes from my background knowledge and is not in the sources.
- **Dated evidence:** The rate data is from May and July 2026 [2][6], and some transfer details come from older pages [4][5]. Check current rates.
- Marcus's same-day transfer limit comes from a single, older source [4].

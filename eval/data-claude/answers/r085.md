**Recommendation: Marcus by Goldman Sachs High-Yield Savings**

Marcus fits your three priorities best. It is also simple enough that you can leave $15,000 there without managing it.

**Why Marcus**
- **No fees or conditions.** CBS News describes it as having no monthly fee, no minimum opening deposit, no minimum balance to earn the full APY, and no cap on withdrawals or transfers [2]. BankBonus likewise lists it as having no fees and no minimum deposit [4].
- **Easy transfers.** You can link external accounts, and same-day transfers are allowed on amounts up to $100,000 [4]. The app is described as intuitive, and an account takes only minutes to open [4]. Customer service is available 24/7 [4].
- **Competitive rate.** It pays 3.50% APY, listed at $365 a year on $10,000 [2]. That is far above the 0.38% national average [2]. Savings rates are variable, so this can change [2].
- **FDIC insurance.** The sources describe high-yield savings accounts as federally insured deposit accounts [2]. Confirm FDIC coverage on Marcus's own site when you open the account, because the sources don't state it for Marcus specifically. $15,000 is well under the standard $250,000 coverage limit cited for FDIC-insured accounts [4].

**Trade-off:** Marcus has no checking account and no ATM access, so it works best as a dedicated savings account linked to your regular bank [2]. For an emergency fund, that separation is often a plus.

**Alternatives**
- **EverBank** pays 3.90% APY with no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees and no required monthly activity. Its app is rated 4.7 stars [6]. It is a good pick if you want a higher rate with no strings.
- **Vio Bank** pays 4.00% on all balances with no monthly fees and a $100 opening deposit. It has no checking account, and you must transfer money to an external bank before you can spend it [6].
- **Synchrony** has no fees or minimums and an optional ATM card, but its rate is 3.40% [2][4].
- **Ally** has no monthly fees and lets you transfer to other banks easily [5]. That review is from 2017, so check current terms and rates.
- **Axos** shows the highest advertised rates, at 4.21% [2] and up to 5.00% [6]. The sources give different figures for it. Both require monthly direct deposits and minimum balances, and the rate drops to 1.00% if you miss them [2][6]. That makes it a poor fit for a simple emergency fund.
- **Varo** also has a high headline rate, but the top rate applies only to the first $5,000 and requires direct deposits [4][6].

**Bottom line:** Choose Marcus for the cleanest no-fee, easy-transfer setup. Choose EverBank if you would rather have a higher rate and can accept less detail on transfers. Check the current APY and FDIC status on the bank's site before funding the account.

**Recommendation: Coursera, specifically the IBM Data Analyst Professional Certificate.**

**Why Coursera fits your two requirements**

- **Recognised certificate.** Coursera partners with well-known universities and companies, and one comparison argues that if you need an accredited certificate, even in data and programming, Coursera is a better match than DataCamp [2]. Another source says platform certificates from Google and IBM carry strong brand recognition, though they are completion-based rather than proctored exams [4].
- **Python focus.** Of the three big Coursera data certificates, IBM's is the Python-focused one. It covers Python, Jupyter notebooks, SQL, Excel, data wrangling and dashboards [1]. Google's is built around R, and Meta's leans toward marketing and statistics [1].
- **Hands-on work.** One learner who is studying it alongside a full-time job describes hands-on labs in the Excel course and a graded dashboard assignment. They also say the first course is the least practical and later ones get more practical [7].
- **Flexibility.** Coursera offers subscriptions such as Coursera Plus, which suits people fitting study around other commitments [6]. edX is mostly pay-per-course and can be more academically demanding and time-consuming [6].

**Timing at 5 hours a week**

Coursera estimates the IBM program at four months at 10 hours a week [1]. At 5 hours a week, expect roughly twice that, about eight months. That is my own arithmetic, not a figure from the sources. The learner in [7] took about a month per course while working full time and being inconsistent. They also note that only around 12% of people finish MOOCs, so a steady weekly routine matters more than speed.

**Alternatives**

- **Dataquest (Beginner Python for Data Analysis).** This is the strongest fit if hands-on projects matter most to you. It teaches pandas, NumPy and visualisation through real datasets and builds portfolio projects. It takes 3 to 6 months at 5 to 10 hours a week and costs $33 to 49 a month. But it is portfolio-based rather than a university-backed certificate [4]. The same source argues hiring managers value what you can build over the credentials you hold [4].
- **DataCamp.** It is described as more hands-on and bite-sized for data topics and can be cheaper. Its certificates are not backed by a university, though [2]. It is a good choice if budget and short lessons come first.
- **edX / Harvard CS50P.** This gives a strong brand name. The certificate is free through Harvard, or $219 as an edX verified certificate. But it teaches general Python, not data analysis, and takes about 100 to 150 hours [4].
- **University of Michigan's Python for Everybody (Coursera).** A popular beginner specialization at roughly $49 to 59 a month [4].

**Practical tip**

If you already know Python basics, skip the broad certificate. Take a shorter course or a Coursera Guided Project, which starts around $9.99 [2]. Then build one or two portfolio projects of your own. Whichever route you choose, look at job postings in your field to see which tools employers actually name [4]. For example, the learner in [7] found Tableau in about 80% of analyst postings they reviewed, while IBM's course teaches Cognos instead.

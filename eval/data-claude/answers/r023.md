I recommend Coursera, specifically the IBM Data Analyst Professional Certificate. Treat it as a provisional pick, because the sources disagree on several points.

**Why Coursera and IBM**
- Coursera partners with well-known universities and companies, and one reviewer says it is the better match if you need an accredited certificate even in data and programming [2]. A second article also rates Coursera's certificates as the more industry-relevant [6].
- Google's certificate is R-focused. IBM's is Python-focused and lists Python, Jupyter notebooks and Python libraries [1].
- It includes hands-on work. A learner on the IBM track describes step-by-step labs using real datasets, though in the Excel course [8]. A single learner's account is thin evidence.
- It is a completion-based certificate, not a proctored exam. Even so, brand-name platform certificates from Google and IBM carry strong recognition [4].
- Hiring managers care more about what you can build than about credentials [4]. Keep your own portfolio projects alongside the certificate.

**The main caveat is time at 5 hours a week**
Coursera estimates the IBM program at 4 months at 10 hours a week [1]. By my own arithmetic that is roughly 8 months at 5 hours a week. One article instead claims Coursera's estimate is about eight months at 10 hours, which would mean well over a year and a half at 5 hours [7]. Coursera's own page [1] is the primary source for its own estimate, so I trust it over [7]. Even so, expect a long haul. Part of the program covers Excel and IBM's proprietary Cognos tool, which one learner says rarely appears in job postings [8]. If you work in marketing, Meta's certificate is a Python option at 5 months at 10 hours a week [1]. It leans toward statistics and marketing analytics [1].

**Where the sources conflict: DataCamp**
Only one source, [7], names DataCamp as the best fit, and it claims DataCamp's certifications are "now accredited" and recognised for academic credit. That claim is uncorroborated. An older source says neither DataCamp nor Udacity is accredited by any institute or university [2]. [2] is from 2024 and [7] from late 2026, so things may have changed. [7] also gives Coursera Plus pricing of $79 a month or $599 a year, while [2] and [6] say $59 a month and about $399 a year. Pricing changes, but [7]'s claims that I could not verify make me cautious about its recommendation. DataCamp does look strong on short, hands-on lessons, since [2] says its method is more hands-on [2]. I could not confirm that its certificate is recognised, so it is a reasonable choice if hands-on practice matters more to you than the credential.

**Other alternatives**
- **Harvard CS50P:** free certificate from Harvard, or $219 for an edX verified certificate. It is general programming of roughly 100 to 150 hours, not data analysis [4][7].
- **Dataquest:** project-based Python for data analysis at $33 to $49 a month, taking 3 to 6 months at 5 to 10 hours a week [4]. Dataquest published that claim, so it has a commercial interest.
- **edX:** more academic, with MicroMasters credentials that can count toward degree credit [6].

**Bottom line:** the evidence on certificate recognition is thin everywhere. Before you pay, check the certificate against job postings in your field [4].

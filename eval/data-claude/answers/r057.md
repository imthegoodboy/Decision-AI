## Recommendation

A certified-used **Trek Allant+ 7**, bought through Upway, is my pick. It ranked first in the evidence-weighted analysis (74.5/100). The evidence for it is thinner than for the other bikes, so treat it as a lead to confirm rather than a settled answer.

## Why

- It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive motor that "pulls cleanly" on hills [6]. Three sources say mid-drives suit hilly riding better than hub motors [3][6][7].
- Hydraulic disc brakes are standard [6]. Hydraulics give more stopping power with less hand effort and self-adjust as pads wear [7].
- Its 500Wh battery is removable, UL certified and described as "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [6]. This is the only source on that point.
- The bikes in that source originally retailed for $2,000 to $3,500, and Upway lists them under $1,500 as inspected used units with a one-year warranty [6]. Source [6] gives no exact Allant+ 7 price.

## Trade-offs

- It is used, not new. A new mid-drive generally isn't available at this price, because bikes under $1,500 mostly use hub motors [4].
- Everything about this bike comes from Upway [6], which sells it. Upway's reliability score is only 57/100.

## Alternatives

- **Ride1Up Turris (new, $1,295):** a 750W hub motor said to climb 10%+ grades, with hydraulic brakes [4]. That source is undated and low in reliability (40/100). It says nothing on battery replacement.
- **Lectric XP4 (new, $999):** a torque sensor and hydraulic brakes [1][5]. Its hill performance was not verified, and a 750W version with a larger battery costs more [1].
- **Avoid for hills:**
  - Aventon Pace 500.3: its 500W motor feels underpowered on a real hill with a load [4].
  - Soltera.2 and Ride1Up Core-5: both have mechanical brakes, and the Soltera.2's smaller 360Wh battery is another drawback [7].
- **Rad Power:** its bankruptcy puts parts and warranty at risk [5]. A separate battery safety warning applies to some older Rad batteries [3].

## Conflicting evidence

The Pace 500.3's price differs by source. Source [4] says $1,399, and an older 2024 Medium post [8] says $1,699. I'd trust neither without checking the current price. Source [4] is also internally inconsistent about the XP 3.0's brakes, which is another reason to weight it lightly.

## Before you decide

- Ask a Trek dealer for the price and stock of the replacement battery. That is the key unverified claim.
- Check the current Upway listing price and the condition of the battery.
- No source gives replacement-battery availability for the new bikes, so ask those manufacturers too.

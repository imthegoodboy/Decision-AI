**Recommendation: EverBank savings account**

EverBank is my pick for a $15,000 emergency fund. It pays 3.90% APY with no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees and no required monthly behavior. Two sources say this [4][7]. One of them states that every account it lists carries federal deposit insurance up to $250,000, so $15,000 is fully covered [4]. Its mobile app is rated 4.7 stars on Google Play and the Apple App Store, and it has some physical branches, mostly in Florida, California and New York [4][7]. I found no source that gives EverBank's transfer speeds, so check its transfer times before you commit. The app rating and branches are the only evidence I have for "easy transfers."

**Why not the highest rates?**
- **Varo.** The sources conflict. One says its 3.75% applies to the whole balance with no cap [4]. Another says 3.75% applies only to the first $5,000, with 1.00% above that [7]. An older page (published 2023) gives yet another structure, 5.00% on the first $5,000 and 2.50% above [5]. All of them require $1,000 or more in monthly direct deposits. I would trust the caps in [7] and [5] more, because they agree that a cap exists. Even so, I wouldn't rely on Varo for $15,000.
- **Axos.** Its 4.21% requires monthly direct deposits plus a balance threshold. If you miss it, the rate falls to 1.00% [2][4][7]. Fortune's text is inconsistent here: the key takeaways say 4.21%, but the Axos entry says 5.00% [7]. Fortune also says Axos offers $2.5 million in expanded FDIC coverage, which I found in no other source [7]. For an emergency fund, a rate that drops when you miss a requirement is a real risk [4].

**Alternatives**
- **Vio Bank:** 4.00% on all balances, no monthly fees, no monthly activity requirements, and $100 to open [4][7]. It has no checking account or debit card, so you must transfer to an external bank before spending [4][7]. It's a good choice if you value rate over convenience.
- **Marcus:** 3.50%, no fees, no minimums [2][4]. The sources disagree on transfers. A 2023-dated page claims same-day transfers [5]. A newer page (November 2026) says no same-day transfers, 3-5 business days to an external account, and $10 per withdrawal after six a month [4]. I trust the newer, more specific source, but I couldn't verify it independently.
- **Ally:** No monthly fee and free standard ACH transfers, but $10 per withdrawal after six a month and no branch or ATM access [4][6]. Note that [6] dates from 2017.
- **Synchrony:** 3.40%, no fees or minimums, optional ATM card [2][4].

**Caveats**
- All rates are variable and can change at any time [2][4]. The sources are dated from May to November 2026, so confirm the current rate.
- A quarter-point rate difference on $15,000 is under $40 a year, so put access and reliability ahead of squeezing out yield [4].
- Gerald [1][3] is a cash-advance app, not a savings account, so I ignored it.

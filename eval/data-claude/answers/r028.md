**My recommendation: EverBank savings, with Vio Bank as the main alternative**

EverBank fits your three priorities best. It pays 3.90% APY with no monthly fees, no minimum opening deposit and no required monthly behavior [4][7]. Fortune calls it one of the most accessible low-maintenance accounts and notes its app has a 4.7-star rating on Google Play and the Apple App Store [7]. It is also one of the few accounts on these lists with physical branches, though they are mostly in Florida, California and New York [4][7]. The sources don't give EverBank's transfer times, so check those before you commit.

Its main advantage for an emergency fund is that the rate has no conditions attached. Some higher-rate accounts drop sharply when you miss a requirement. Axos advertises up to 4.21% but falls to a 1.00% base rate if you miss the direct-deposit and balance requirements in a month [2][4]. One review warns against rates that depend on a balance you may need to spend down just when an emergency hits [4]. Every account in the main review carries FDIC insurance up to $250,000, so $15,000 is fully covered [4]. All rates are variable and can change at any time [2][4].

**Alternatives**
- **Vio Bank:** It pays 4.00% APY on all balances, with no monthly fees or activity requirements, but you need $100 to open it [4][7]. The tradeoff is access. It has no checking account, debit card or branches, so you must transfer to an outside bank before spending [4][7]. That is acceptable if you already have a checking account.
- **Marcus by Goldman Sachs:** It has no fees or minimums and pays 3.50% [2][4]. Sources disagree on transfer speed. One says external transfers take three to five business days and withdrawals beyond six a month cost $10 [4]. An older article says same-day transfers are available [5]. Because speed matters to you, I would rank it lower.
- **Synchrony:** It has no fees or minimums, pays 3.40%, and offers an optional ATM card [2][4]. LendingClub's LevelUp also includes a no-fee ATM card, but it needs $250 deposited each cycle to earn 4.00% instead of 3.00% [2][4][7].
- **Ally:** It has no monthly fee and free standard ACH transfers, plus a Buckets feature for labeling emergency money. It charges $10 per withdrawal after six a month and has no ATM access [4][6].

**Accounts I would skip for $15,000:** Varo is described inconsistently. One source says 3.75% applies to the whole balance, and another says it applies only to the first $5,000, with 1.00% above that [4][7]. Both say it requires $1,000 in monthly direct deposits. With that much money, the cap could matter a lot.

**Bottom line:** The rate gap between these accounts is small. A quarter-point difference on $15,000 is under $40 a year [4]. Choose on access and simplicity, and open EverBank, or Vio if you want the slightly higher rate and already have a checking account.

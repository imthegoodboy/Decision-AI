## Recommendation

Amazfit Balance ($199) is my pick, with low confidence. It is the only option under $200 where a source reports a test of heart-rate accuracy. No source I have measures sleep accuracy against a reference device, so no tracker can be called "most accurate" for sleep.

The tool's evidence-weighted ranking put Fitbit Air first (68/100) and Balance second (66.5/100), which is effectively a tie. I still favour Balance because the Air scored well mainly on price and subscription terms. None of the sources I verified tests the Air's heart-rate accuracy.

## Why

- Live Science says the Balance's heart rate and body composition sensors were "top-class in our tests" [1].
- The same review says it "accurately measured" heart rate, breathing rate and heart rate variability [1].
- It costs $199 and is often on sale [1].
- Only extras, such as an AI coach chatbot, sit behind a paywall, and the reviewer calls them non-essential [1].

## Trade-offs

- The heart-rate accuracy claim rests on a single review [1]. The page is dated 2022, though it lists 2026 picks.
- The review praises the Balance for sleep hygiene and stress management [1], but it gives no sleep-accuracy result I could verify.
- At $199 there is no price margin.
- The display is not Gorilla Glass, so it is a poor fit for extreme outdoor use [1].

## Alternatives

- **Fitbit Air ($99).** It is subscription-free for primary features [2]. Tom's Guide says its sleep reports are on par with some of the best smartwatches [2], but that is a judgement about report detail, not a measured accuracy test. It has no onboard GPS or altimeter [2]. Optional Google Health Premium costs $9.99 a month for AI insights [2].
- **Garmin vívosmart 5.** Popular Science lists "accurate heart rate and sleep tracking" and says Garmin does not charge extra for your data [7]. That review is from 2023 and gives no price in the retrieved text, so check the current price.
- **Amazfit Bip 6 Pro.** One thin source says it includes heart rate, SpO2 and sleep tracking without a subscription [5]. It gives no test data or price, so treat it as weak.
- **Xiaomi Band 7 Pro.** Popular Science says its sleep tracking "isn't accurate" [7].

## Conflicting evidence

Popular Science says you need Fitbit Premium for "detailed sleep data" on the Inspire 3 [7]. Tom's Guide says the Air's detailed sleep reports are free, and only AI-backed trend recommendations need Premium [2]. These are different devices from different years, so they may not truly conflict. I trust Tom's Guide more here: it is newer (2026), and the tool rates it higher after verification (77, against 59 for Popular Science). Fitbit's free-versus-paid line has clearly shifted, so check what the current version of any Fitbit locks.

Separately, Terra's data from thousands of users shows Garmin's sleep data is the most consistent, and Fitbit's is also clean [6]. This measures consistency and outliers, not accuracy against a lab reference. It covers whole brands, not the specific models above.

## Before you decide

- Confirm current prices and any new paywalls, since the sources span 2022 to 2026.
- Look for independent validation of sleep staging against polysomnography. None of these sources provides it.
- If sleep matters more than heart-rate accuracy, the Fitbit Air at $99 is a reasonable cheaper choice, given the near-tie in the ranking.

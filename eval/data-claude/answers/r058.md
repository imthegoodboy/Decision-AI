**Recommendation: Ride1Up Turris (about $1,295)**

For a hilly 10-mile commute, the Turris is the best match among these sources. Two caveats up front: the sources disagree on some brake specs, and the Turris's battery-replacement path is weaker than I'd like.

**Why the Turris**
- **Hills:** It has a 750W rear hub motor, and one review says it climbs 10%+ grades "without begging for help" [4]. A second review calls it "the strongest climber on paper" among the sub-$1,500 commuters it tested [6].
- **Brakes:** Both sources list Tektro hydraulic discs [4][6]. Hydraulics give more stopping power with less hand effort and self-adjust as pads wear, which helps on heavy bikes descending every day [6][8].
- **Battery:** The 48V 14Ah pack (672Wh) is well above the roughly 450Wh a 20-mile hilly round trip calls for [6]. One reviewer reported 35–40 real-world miles at assist level 2 [4].

**The catch: battery replacement**
Ride1Up sells direct-to-consumer only, so there is no local dealer to diagnose a fault or swap a battery. You would email support and wait for shipped parts [6]. That is a real compromise on your "easily available battery replacement" requirement. Before buying, I'd confirm that Ride1Up sells replacement packs and what they cost.

**Alternatives**
- **Rad Mission 2 ($1,099):** One review picks it as the best hill commuter in the bracket. It cites hydraulic 180mm discs, Rad's large dealer and service network, and easier access to replacement packs [6]. However, another source lists the Mission 2 with mechanical discs [8], so verify the brakes before buying. It also has a cadence sensor, a 500W motor and a single speed [6]. Rad's recent bankruptcy raises warranty and parts risk, and the CPSC warned about certain older Rad batteries. Buy new from an authorized dealer [3][6].
- **Aventon Pace 500.3 (about $1,399):** It has hydraulic brakes, a torque sensor and Aventon's large bike-shop footprint [6]. Its 500W motor feels underpowered on a real hill with a full bag [4][6], so it suits moderate hills better.
- **Used Trek Allant+ 7 (under $1,500 on Upway):** It has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive (85 Nm), hydraulic brakes, and a UL-certified 500Wh battery "available for purchase anywhere in the US" [7]. That is the strongest answer on both hills and battery availability. Inventory is unpredictable, though, and you inherit someone else's battery [6].
- **Lectric XP4 ($999):** I'd skip it for this job. One source credits it with a custom hydraulic brake system [1], but another says the base model ships with mechanical discs and labors on sustained grades [6].

**Bottom line**
Choose the Turris if climbing power and braking matter most and you can live with mail-order support. If local battery and service access matters more, look for a used Trek Allant+ 7, or confirm the Mission 2's brakes first.

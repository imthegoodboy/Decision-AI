## Recommendation
Open an **EverBank** savings account. It ranked first in the evidence-weighted analysis (78/100), ahead of Vio Bank (73/100).

## Why
- EverBank pays 3.90% APY with no minimum opening deposit, no monthly fees and no required monthly behaviour, so the full $15,000 earns the rate [7][4].
- Its mobile app is rated 4.7 stars on Google Play and the Apple App Store, which helps with day-to-day transfers [7][4]. It also has some branches, mostly in Florida, California and New York [4][7].
- FDIC insurance: one source says every account it lists carries coverage up to $250,000, so $15,000 would be fully covered [4]. Only that source says so explicitly (see Before you decide).
- All rates are variable and can change at any time [4][2]. A quarter-point difference on $15,000 is under $40 a year [4], so the fee and access terms matter more than a few hundredths of a percent.

## Trade-offs
- EverBank's 3.90% is slightly below Vio's 4.00% [7].
- None of the sources states EverBank's transfer speed, so "easy transfers" rests on its app rating and branches, not on documented timing.

## Alternatives
- **Vio Bank:** 4.00% APY on all balances, no monthly fees or activity requirements, but $100 to open [7][4]. It has no checking account and no branches, so you must transfer to an external bank before you can spend the money [7][4]. It suits you if you already have a checking account elsewhere.
- **Axos Bank:** up to 4.21%, but the rate falls to a 1.00% base if you miss the direct-deposit and balance requirements [4][2][7]. That is a poor fit for an emergency fund.
- **LendingClub:** 4.00% if you deposit at least $250 each month, otherwise 3.00% [7][4][2].
- **Ally:** no monthly fee, but $10 per withdrawal after six in a month, and no branch or ATM access [4][6].

## Conflicting evidence
- **Varo:** The Steady Dollar Review says 3.75% applies to the entire balance with no cap [4]. Fortune says it applies only to the first $5,000, with 1.00% above that [7]. Fortune is dated July 2026 and scored higher on reliability (78, versus 64 for Steady Dollar). On that basis, treat Varo as a poor fit for $15,000.
- **Marcus:** The Steady Dollar Review says there are no same-day transfers (3 to 5 business days) and a limit of six withdrawals a month [4]. BankBonus says same-day transfers up to $100,000 [5]. CBS says there is no cap on withdrawals or transfers [2]. BankBonus is flagged as possibly outdated (published 2023), but CBS (May 2026) is no newer than the Steady Dollar Review (November 2026). I could not resolve this, so I did not rely on Marcus.

## Before you decide
- Confirm the FDIC status of EverBank (or Vio) on the FDIC's BankFind tool, because only one source states it.
- Check the current APY and EverBank's transfer terms, because rates change and no source gives transfer timing.
- The sources are rate roundups dated May to November 2026, not bank disclosures.

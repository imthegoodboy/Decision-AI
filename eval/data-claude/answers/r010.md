I recommend the **Sony ULT WEAR**, with some caveats because the sources disagree.

**Why the ULT WEAR**
- SoundGuys, which tests with a lab measurement rig, picked it as best overall under $200. It also won its Best ANC, Best Over-Ear Headphones and Best for Comfort awards [6].
- It measured more than 25dB of attenuation above 80Hz, which SoundGuys calls well suited to commutes [6]. Low-frequency noise is what train rumble is, and ANC handles it best [5].
- Battery isn't a worry. It lasted 32 hours 35 minutes in testing, and it supports multipoint and a 3.5mm cable [6].
- Its bass boost is heavy, but you can tame it in the app EQ [6]. That matters little to you, since bass is a low priority.

**Where the sources conflict**
- Platform & Pocket says the ULT WEAR's low-frequency attenuation barely reaches 15dB [8]. That contradicts SoundGuys' lab figure [6]. I trust the lab measurement more, but audition them if you can.
- Platform & Pocket's top pick is the JBL Live 670NC, which it calls a full over-ear design [8]. SoundGuys describes the same model as on-ear [6]. That matters for you, because Which? notes that not all on-ear headphones are comfortable for long sessions [7].
- Its ANC measurement is strong (close to 30dB around 250Hz), and battery was 54 hours 57 minutes [6]. If you don't mind on-ear, it's a credible budget option, but I wouldn't buy it as an over-ear without checking.
- The sources I read give prices in dollars, not pounds. Check current UK prices before buying.

**Alternatives**
- **Sony WH-1000XM5:** What Hi-Fi? rated their noise cancelling among the class leaders for isolating you from train and Tube drone, and their comfort as up there with the best. They weigh 250g [1]. They launched at £380 but are now superseded, so they're worth buying only if discounted to about £200 [1][8]. Platform & Pocket claims about 320g [8], which conflicts with What Hi-Fi?'s 250g figure [1]. They also only fold flat, so the case takes up bag space [1].
- **Anker Soundcore Space One:** SoundGuys picked it as best for Android users. It folds, feels lightweight, and lasted 42 hours 57 minutes with ANC on [6]. It supports LDAC and has adjustable ANC strength [6].
- **Soundcore Space 2:** Anker's own guide pitches it as the comfort-first option, with a pressure-relieving headband and soft cushions [5]. That is a manufacturer claim, so treat it with caution.
- **JLab JBuds Lux ANC:** This is the budget pick. Its ANC is respectable but not outstanding, at 10–20dB below 700Hz [6].
- **Bose QuietComfort Ultra:** It cancels even more noise than the Sony XM5 [1], but it launched at £450, well over your budget [1].

**Bottom line:** Start with the ULT WEAR. If you find them for about £200 or less, the XM5 are the better pick for comfort and ANC. Check the return policy either way, because comfort in the second hour is hard to judge in a shop.

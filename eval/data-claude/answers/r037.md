**Recommendation: Garmin vívosmart 5, with the Fitbit Air as a close alternative**

The Garmin vívosmart 5 is the best fit for your three requirements. Popular Science's top pick among cheap trackers lists "accurate heart rate and sleep tracking" as a pro. It also lists "free access to all your data" [7]. The review says Garmin doesn't charge extra for data or training-assistance features, which is why it took the top spot [7]. It monitors heart rate continuously and tracks sleep, stress and respiration [7]. The review doesn't give a price, so confirm it is under $200 before you buy.

Garmin's data also holds up in a larger comparison. Terra analysed sleep records from thousands of users and found Garmin produced the most consistent sleep data, with the lowest variability and very low outlier rates [6]. Caveats: that study compares platforms across mixed hardware and users, not this specific band. It also says nothing about heart-rate accuracy [6]. The Popular Science review dates from 2023, so check that the model is still sold [7].

Drawbacks: the vívosmart 5 has no built-in GPS and relies on your phone for location [7].

**Alternatives**

- **Fitbit Air ($99):** This screenless tracker is subscription-free for core features. Tom's Guide says its sleep reports are on par with some of the best smartwatches [2], and that it is as reliable at logging sleep trends as Garmin watches costing 2.5 times as much [2]. It tracks heart rate around the clock [2]. Some features, such as AI sleep-trend recommendations, need Google Health Premium at $9.99 a month or $99 a year [2]. That makes it subscription-optional, not subscription-free. It also has no onboard GPS [2]. Terra found Fitbit data to be clean and balanced, with sensible nap detection [6].
- **Amazfit Balance ($199):** Live Science's top overall pick. Its testers found heart rate, breathing rate, HRV and body composition measurements accurate. It has up to two weeks of battery life [1]. The catch is that some features, such as the AI coach, sit behind a paywall. Live Science calls these non-essential [1].
- **Fitbit Inspire 3:** Popular Science's "best for sleep" pick. But detailed sleep data, wellness reports and the Daily Readiness Score require Fitbit Premium [7]. That makes it a weaker match for you.

**What to avoid:** Whoop requires a pricey subscription [7] and costs $199 to $359 a year [2]. Popular Science says the Xiaomi Band 7 Pro's sleep tracking isn't accurate [7].

**Bottom line:** Pick the vívosmart 5 if you want accuracy and no strings attached. Pick the Fitbit Air if you prefer a tiny, comfortable band and can ignore the optional Premium upsell.

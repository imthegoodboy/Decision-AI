## Recommendation
Wealthfront is my pick for a $5,000 beginner who cares most about fees and taxes. Betterment is a close second. The evidence-weighted ranking from the verification tool agrees: Wealthfront 81.5, Betterment 74, Schwab 39.5. Vanguard Digital Advisor could not be scored because the evidence is thin and conflicting.

## Why
- Wealthfront's core service costs 0.25% a year with a $500 minimum, so $5,000 clears the bar easily [3]. Several other sources give the same figures [2][4][5][6].
- Wealthfront is said to harvest tax losses daily and to choose replacement funds that avoid wash sales [2]. This rests on one low-reliability source, so treat it as likely but unconfirmed.
- Betterment's standard fee is also 0.25% [7]. It offers tax-loss harvesting on taxable accounts at all balance levels [2], and NerdWallet lists it with a $10 minimum [1]. That is a strong tax feature at your balance.
- Schwab Intelligent Portfolios has no advisory fee and a $5,000 minimum [5]. But tax-loss harvesting there requires $50,000 [1], so it does not help you on the tax side.

## Trade-offs
- Betterment may charge a flat $5/month at low balances. That is about a 6% annual equivalent on $1,000 [2], and roughly 1.2% a year on $5,000. Source [2] is low-reliability, though [1] shows "0.25% or $5/month" and [8] shows "$4/mo., or 0.25%". The conditions for the flat fee are unclear, so check them before you sign up.
- Schwab's "free" service has a cost. It holds a share of your money in cash, which acts as an implicit fee [2][4].
- Tax-loss harvesting only helps in taxable accounts, not in IRAs [2].

## Alternatives
- **Betterment:** choose it if you want goal-based tools or a lower minimum, and the flat-fee check comes out fine [1][2].
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios:** choose it only if you already bank or invest at Schwab and do not need tax-loss harvesting [4][5].
- **Fidelity Go:** one blog reports $0 fees under $10,000 and 0.35% above [5]. NerdWallet puts its tax-loss harvesting minimum at $25,000 [1].
- **Robinhood Strategies:** it has a $3,000 minimum for tax-loss harvesting [1].
- **Acorns:** it has high fixed fees for small balances [8].

## Conflicting evidence
- **Wealthfront direct indexing minimum.** Source [6] says $5,000, citing a Form ADV. Sources [2], [4] and [5] say $100,000+, and [3] says $500,000. NerdWallet [1] only says Wealthfront's minimum is "much lower" than the usual six-figure level. I could not resolve this. Do not count on direct indexing at $5,000.
- **Schwab cash allocation.** Source [2] says 6-10%. Source [4] says 6-30%. Both agree it is a real drag.
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor and tax-loss harvesting.** NerdWallet [1] lists it as offering automated tax-loss harvesting. Source [2] says it does not. I trust [1] more because it has a named author, a clear date and a higher reliability score. Because of the conflict, I did not use this claim.

## Before you decide
- Confirm current fees and minimums on each provider's site. Most of these sources are blogs or aggregators, and the NerdWallet review dates from October 2025.
- Ask Betterment exactly when the flat monthly fee applies.
- Decide whether the money goes into a taxable account. If it goes into an IRA, tax-loss harvesting is irrelevant and low fees matter more.

**Recommendation: Coursera, using the IBM Data Analyst Professional Certificate (or a shorter Python-focused Coursera programme).**

**Why Coursera**

- You want a recognised certificate. Coursera partners with well-known universities and companies, and one reviewer says it is the better match if you need an accredited certificate, even in data and programming [2]. Platform certificates from Harvard, Google and IBM carry strong brand recognition, though they are completion-based rather than proctored exams [4].
- Python is the key. Of the three big Coursera data certificates, IBM's is the Python-focused one. It is aimed at people interested in the technical side of analysis, and it covers Python, Jupyter notebooks, libraries, SQL and Excel [1]. Google's certificate centres on R, so it is a weaker fit if you specifically want Python [1]. Meta's is Python with a statistics and marketing emphasis [1].
- Hands-on work is built in. Coursera offers guided projects as well as courses [6]. A learner on the IBM track describes hands-on labs, and notes that later courses get more practical than the dry first one [7].
- Flexibility suits a job. Coursera's subscription model is described as better for people balancing study with other commitments [6]. Coursera Plus is about $399 a year, and individual courses cost $49-$79 [6].

**Be realistic about 5 hours a week**

Coursera lists the IBM certificate at about 4 months at 10 hours a week [1]. At 5 hours a week, expect roughly 8 months. A learner combining it with a full-time job found each course took about a month, and she cites a figure that only around 12% of people finish MOOCs [7]. A guide on Python certificates warns that a shorter certificate you will finish beats a comprehensive one you will abandon [4]. If 8 months feels too long, the University of Michigan "Python for Everybody" Specialization is on Coursera at roughly $49-59 a month [4].

**Alternatives**

- **edX:** This is better for academic depth. It is backed by universities such as Harvard and MIT, and its MicroMasters credentials can count toward degree credit [2][6]. It has fewer practical, industry-oriented certificates than Coursera [6]. Harvard's CS50P gives a free Harvard certificate, or $219 for an edX verified one. It teaches Python fundamentals rather than data analysis, and it takes about 100-150 hours [4].
- **DataCamp:** It is described as more hands-on and bite-sized, and potentially cheaper [2]. However, it is not accredited by any institute or university [2]. That makes it weaker for your certificate requirement.
- **Dataquest:** Its Python for Data Analysis path is built around real-dataset projects, at $33-49 a month and 5-10 hours a week [4]. It gives you portfolio projects rather than a brand-name certificate. The same guide says hiring managers value what you can build over credentials [4].
- **Udacity:** It offers graded and capstone projects plus mentorship, but it is expensive and not accredited [2].

**Bottom line**

Take the IBM Data Analyst certificate on Coursera for the recognised name and Python focus. Add your own project built from real work data, since portfolio projects matter to employers [4]. Preview the first module for free before paying [1]. If you want a lighter start, try Python for Everybody first.

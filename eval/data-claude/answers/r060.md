## Recommendation

A certified pre-owned **Trek Allant+ 7** is the best fit for a hilly 10-mile commute under $1,500. It is the only option that meets all three of your priorities in the sources. If you want a new bike, the **Ride1Up Turris ($1,295)** is the runner-up. This ranking matches the evidence-weighted ranking from the verification tool (Allant+ 7 first, Turris second). Confidence is moderate, because the Allant+ evidence comes mostly from one used-bike retailer.

## Why

- **Hills:** The Allant+ 7 has a Bosch Performance Line CX mid-drive with 85Nm of torque, which the retailer says pulls cleanly on hills [7]. Another source says mid-drives handle hills more naturally [6].
- **Brakes:** Hydraulic discs are standard on it [7]. Hydraulics give more stopping power with less hand effort and self-adjust as pads wear [8].
- **Battery:** Its 500Wh removable battery is UL certified and sold anywhere in the US [7]. A second source says the same [6].

## Trade-offs

- The bike is used, and Upway's seller says inventory is unpredictable [6]. You also inherit someone else's battery [6].
- The Allant+ brake and battery claims come from a single retailer [7]. Treat that as thin evidence, not proof.

## Alternatives

- **Ride1Up Turris ($1,295, new):** It has a 750W hub motor, a 672Wh battery and Tektro hydraulic discs [6]. A second source agrees [4]. That source says it climbs 10%+ grades [4], but this is a single-source claim from a low-reliability site. The weak point is support. Ride1Up sells direct only, so there is no local dealer to swap a battery and you wait on shipped parts [6].
- **Aventon Pace 500.3 (~$1,400):** It has hydraulic brakes, but its 500W motor feels underpowered on a real hill with a full bag [4][6]. I would skip it for your route.
- **Aventon Soltera.2:** It has mechanical brakes and a 360Wh battery, and is built for flat commutes [6][8].
- **Rad Mission 2 (~$1,099):** I would not rely on it for the reasons below.

## Conflicting evidence

- **Rad Mission 2 brakes:** Source [6] says hydraulic discs on 180mm rotors, but source [8]'s table says mechanical disc, 180mm. I could not resolve this, so I did not use the claim. [6] ranks the Mission 2 first for hills, but its own reliability score fell to 64 after verification, and it also contradicts other sources on the XP4 brakes.
- **Lectric XP4 base-model brakes:** [6] says the $999 base model has mechanical brakes. Sources [1] and [5] say hydraulic. [1] is an independent tester with hands-on experience and [5] agrees, so I lean towards hydraulic. Check the exact trim before buying.
- **Rad dealer network:** [6] and [8] say Rad has the largest dealer network, which would make battery replacement easy. [5] and [6] also report a recent bankruptcy, which [5] says could affect warranty and parts. There was also a CPSC warning on certain older Rad batteries, though Rad says its Safe Shield and semi-integrated batteries are not covered [3]. Rad's battery availability is therefore uncertain.

## Before you decide

- Check live Upway stock for the Allant+ 7 and ask about battery health and warranty.
- Confirm the brake type on any specific bike, especially the Mission 2 and XP4.
- Check the current status of Rad Power's support before considering a Rad.

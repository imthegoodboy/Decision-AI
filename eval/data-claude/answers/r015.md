## Recommendation

Take **PTE Academic UKVI**, provided your university lists it as acceptable. It is the only option here with sourced evidence of fast results, and it is an approved UK visa test. If your university prefers IELTS, take **IELTS for UKVI**. Both are approved for UK study [6][1][2].

## Why

- The Home Office only accepts Secure English Language Tests (SELTs) from approved providers [1][7]. PTE Academic UKVI is one of them, and the UKVI Academic versions of both PTE and IELTS are accepted for UK study [1][6].
- On speed, PTE Academic UKVI is described as giving quick results, often within 48 hours [1]. Pearson reports that 85.7% of PTE Academic takers got results within 2 days in 2018 [3]. That figure is dated, self-reported and not specific to the UKVI version.
- Some PTE scores take longer when flagged for additional review or security checks [3].
- Your SELT result must have been awarded in the two years before you apply [7].
- IELTS for UKVI uses the same papers as standard IELTS. The difference is the booking and the approved centre [7].

## Trade-offs

- PTE is computer-only. IELTS offers paper or computer in many locations [6].
- None of the sources gives IELTS result times, so I cannot compare speed directly. PTE's speed advantage is plausible but not proven here.

## Alternatives

- **IELTS for UKVI** is suitable for student visas [1]. It is the better pick if you want paper format or your university names it.
- **LanguageCert** is also on the approved SELT list as of September 2026 [7]. It suits you only if your university accepts it.
- **TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test and Cambridge C1** are not SELTs. Some universities accept them because they may assess English themselves [7][2]. Duolingo cannot be used where the Home Office asks for a SELT [7]. They are the riskiest choice for a visa-driven plan.

## Conflicting evidence

IDP, which co-owns IELTS, says IELTS is accepted by over 13,500 organisations against about 3,000 for PTE, and that IELTS is favoured by most [6]. IRO instead calls both tests "widely accepted and trusted" [1]. Sheffield's list includes both [2]. I rate IDP's claim as weaker. It has a commercial interest, gives no sources for its figures, and counts acceptance worldwide rather than for UK visas. For a UK student visa, both are approved. IDP's point matters mainly if your university's own list is narrow.

## Before you decide

- Check your university's English requirements page for accepted tests and scores. Check GOV.UK for the level your route needs. Source [7] says degree-level study needs CEFR B2.
- Book the UKVI version at an approved test centre [1].
- Confirm current IELTS and PTE turnaround times from the official providers.
- Evidence is thin. Most sources are low-reliability or vendor pages, and several are duplicates.

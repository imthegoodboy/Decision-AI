## Recommendation
Wealthfront is my pick for a $5,000 beginner who cares most about fees and taxes. Betterment is a close second. The evidence-weighted ranking was Wealthfront 77.5, Betterment 70, E*TRADE Core Portfolios 63 and Schwab Intelligent Portfolios 38.5.

## Why
- Wealthfront charges a flat 0.25% annual fee [5]. Several other sources give the same figure [2][3][6].
- Its $500 minimum is easily met with $5,000 [2][3][5]. One source says it has no monthly minimums [2].
- It is described as doing daily tax-loss harvesting with a large ETF universe for replacement funds [2]. This comes from a lower-reliability source, so treat it as likely rather than certain.
- Schwab Intelligent Portfolios has no management fee [2][5]. But its tax-loss harvesting needs a $50,000 balance [1][2], so a $5,000 account gets no tax benefit there.

## Trade-offs
- Betterment's fee is 0.25%, but NerdWallet lists "0.25% or $5/month" [1]. One source says the flat fee applies below about $25,000 [2], and another says $4 to $5 a month under $20,000 to $24,000 [7]. That could be well over 1% a year on $5,000 (my arithmetic: $60 on $5,000). No source I read says how to avoid it, so check Betterment's pricing page.
- Wealthfront's tax advantage is mostly about its harvesting engine. The tax benefit of harvesting generally grows with wealth [6], so expect modest savings at $5,000.
- The reviews I read are mostly affiliate-style or blog content, and my one fully independent source, NerdWallet [1], is partly truncated.

## Alternatives
- **Betterment:** Choose it if you want goal-based tools or a near-zero minimum ($10 per NerdWallet [1], $0 per others [2][3]). It offers tax-loss harvesting at all balance levels [2], which NerdWallet's list supports [1].
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios:** It makes sense mainly if you already use Schwab, or for an IRA where harvesting doesn't apply. It needs exactly $5,000 to open [2][5]. It holds a cash allocation as an implicit cost [2][4].
- **E*TRADE Core Portfolios:** It costs 0.30%, has a $500 minimum and offers automated harvesting [1].
- **Others:** Fidelity Go requires $25,000 for harvesting [1]. Robinhood Strategies requires $3,000 [1].

## Conflicting evidence
- **Schwab cash drag:** One source says 6-10% of the portfolio is held in cash [2]. Another says the mandatory cash is 6-30% [4]. Both agree a cash drag exists. The size of the effective cost is uncertain.
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor:** NerdWallet lists it as offering automated harvesting [1]. A less reliable, undated source says it doesn't [2]. NerdWallet is more credible, but I wouldn't rely on either without checking Vanguard.
- **Wealthfront direct indexing:** One source says it unlocks at $5,000 [6]. Others say $100K [2][4][5] or $500K [3]. This doesn't affect a $5,000 decision.

## Before you decide
- Decide whether the money goes in a taxable account or an IRA. Harvesting only helps in taxable accounts [2].
- Confirm current fees, minimums and harvesting eligibility on Wealthfront's and Betterment's own sites, since these change.

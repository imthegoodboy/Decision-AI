**Recommendation: TVS iQube, choosing a bigger-battery variant.**

**Why the iQube**
- Autocar India's 1,000 km comparison, ridden in Mumbai, calls the iQube and Chetak easy-to-ride city commuters. It rates the TVS "a step above the Bajaj" on performance [1]. Its power delivery is smooth and won't overwhelm a new EV rider [1].
- It has good ergonomics and a seat that suits two people [1]. 91Wheels lists strong build quality, a plush ride and a usable Eco mode as pros [8].
- Range: 91Wheels says the bigger-battery variants "offer decent range for daily use" [8]. Claimed ranges run from about 121 to 212 km depending on variant [8]. Your 20 km commute (about 40 km round trip) is well within the larger packs, even after allowing for traffic.
- Price is about ₹95,000 to ₹1.30 lakh [8]. Listed cons are no fast charging and a portable charger that eats into the under-seat storage [8].
- Limits of the evidence: my sources contain no Bengaluru-specific reliability or service-network data for the iQube, Ather or Chetak. The iQube pick rests on TVS being an established manufacturer plus its test results. Before buying, check how close the nearest service centres are to your home and office.

**Alternatives**
- **Bajaj Chetak:** a close second. It has a wide, comfortable seat, a firm yet absorbent ride, and an automatic Eco-to-Sport switch for overtakes [1]. Its top speed is lower, at 69 kph indicated [1]. The Chetak C35 costs about ₹1.24 to ₹1.52 lakh with a 153 km claimed range [8].
- **Ather 450X or 450S:** the best handling and a very usable Eco mode [1]. Autocar found it cramped for taller riders, with a harsh low-speed ride on poor roads [1]. Bengaluru roads are rough, so test-ride it. The 450S is listed at about ₹1.28 to ₹1.69 lakh [8].
- **Ola S1:** I would avoid it if service matters most. Ola is Bengaluru-based and has about 430 service centres. In 2024 it was receiving around 80,000 complaints a month, with some customers waiting 30 to 45 days for repairs [3][5]. Ola later announced a HyperService plan with a one-day resolution promise and 1,000 centres [7]. My sources don't show whether it delivered. Autocar also found the S1 Pro's Eco mode too weak for city traffic [1].
- **Ampere (Magnus Neo or G Max):** worth a look as a budget option. Ampere's own guide claims 85 to 100+ km real-world range, LFP batteries and 420+ service points, with "strong Bangalore coverage" [2]. That is vendor material, so verify it independently.

**Caveats**
The Autocar and RushLane comparisons date from 2022 [1][4][6]. Models, prices and software have changed since, so use them as general character guides. Test-ride your shortlist, and ask owners in your area about service turnaround.

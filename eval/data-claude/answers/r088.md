**Recommendation: Wealthfront**

For a $5,000 beginner who cares most about fees and taxes, Wealthfront looks like the best fit. Its fee is a flat 0.25% a year with no tiers and a $500 minimum, so $5,000 clears the bar easily [2]. NerdWallet's list shows Betterment as "0.25% or $5/month" [1], and another review says Betterment charges a flat $5 a month on balances under $25,000 [2]. By my arithmetic, $5 a month on $5,000 is about $60 a year. That is 1.2%, compared with roughly $12.50 a year at Wealthfront's 0.25%. Check Betterment's current fee terms before you decide, because the sources differ on the thresholds [7].

Wealthfront also leads on taxes. Reviewers describe its tax-loss harvesting as among the most sophisticated, running daily with a wide pool of replacement ETFs to avoid wash sales [2]. Sources disagree on when its direct indexing, which holds individual stocks so losses can be harvested at stock level, becomes available. One says $100,000 or more [2][5]. Another cites Wealthfront's SEC filing for a $5,000 minimum on its S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 direct-indexing products [6]. Confirm this with Wealthfront before relying on it. Tax-loss harvesting only helps in taxable accounts, not IRAs [2].

**Alternatives**

- **Betterment** is the easiest onboarding, with a $0 minimum and goal-based tools [2][3]. It offers tax-loss harvesting at all balance levels [2]. It is the better pick if you want simplicity over the lowest fee, or have well under $5,000 to start.
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios** charges no management fee, but its $5,000 minimum is the highest on the list [2]. It also holds 6–10% of the portfolio in low-yield cash, which acts as a hidden cost [2]. Its tax-loss harvesting requires $50,000 [1][2], so it fails your tax criterion at this balance. It mainly suits existing Schwab customers [4].
- **Vanguard Digital Advisor** costs about 0.15% and has a $100 minimum [2]. Sources disagree on tax-loss harvesting. One says it has none [2], while NerdWallet lists it among robo-advisors with automated harvesting [1]. It suits tax-advantaged accounts such as IRAs, where harvesting doesn't apply anyway [2].
- **Fidelity Go** charges $0 on balances under $10,000, and one source says 0.35% above that [5]. Tax-loss harvesting there requires $25,000 [1], so it is the cheapest option if taxes are not a priority.

**Caveats**

Several sources are blogs or affiliate-style comparison pages, and they disagree on details. NerdWallet's Betterment fee listing is more reliable than the blogs [1]. Verify current pricing on each provider's site. At $5,000 the tax benefit is small. Fees will matter more than harvesting in the early years, which is why Wealthfront's low flat fee is the deciding factor.

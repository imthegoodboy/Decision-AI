**Recommendation: Eureka Forbes 355 (about ₹9,999)**

It meets your first two priorities, with some caveats on the third. Two sources list a 355 m³/h CADR and a HEPA H13 filter with pre-filter and carbon stages, at ₹9,999 [1][2]. That is above the roughly 235 m³/h that the two-thirds rule of thumb gives for 350 sq ft [1][2]. It is also above the 300 m³/h that [2] suggests as a realistic target for Delhi. Both sources agree on the specifications. Note that [1] bases its picks on advertised specs and did no hands-on testing.

**Drawbacks**
- It is loud. It is rated at 63 dB, and [2] calls the comfort zone for a bedroom under 30 dB. Both [1] and [2] flag the noise.
- Its spec sheet lists a plasma stage, and [2] warns that ionising features can produce ozone.
- I found no model-specific replacement-filter price for it. The only figure is the general range of ₹1,500 to ₹5,000 a year [1][2]. Check local filter availability and price before you buy, as both sources advise.

**Alternatives**
- **Qubo Q600 (₹14,490):** It has a 450 m³/h CADR and an H13 filter, but it is about 55 dB [1][2]. It is at the top of your budget, and I found no filter cost for it. Source [2] says it is built for large open-plan areas.
- **Realme TechLife (₹7,999, or ₹7,499 on Flipkart):** Both [2] and the review [7] list a 330 m³/h CADR. The sources disagree on its filter. Source [2] says it uses a True HEPA H13 filter made by Sharp. The hands-on review says the filter is HEPA H12, which it describes as removing 99.95% of particles [7]. I trust [7] more, because it is a review of the product that describes the unit it used. Source [2] gives no basis for its H13 claim. However, [7] dates from 2021, so the specification or price may have changed. If H13 is a must, treat Realme as not meeting it. It is also very loud at 64 dB on top speed [2][7]. Its filter indicator trips at 2,800 hours [2][7], but I found no filter price.
- **Honeywell Air Touch V5 (about ₹10,199):** It is quiet, at about 22 dB [1][2]. The sources conflict on its CADR. Source [1] says 380 m³/h and [2] says 250 m³/h. I cannot resolve this from the sources, so check the CADR on the box or the manufacturer's page. If it is 250, [2] considers it undersized for your room.
- **Mi Air Purifier 4:** It has a 387 m³/h CADR [1][2]. Its price is disputed. Source [1] gives ₹14,999 on Flipkart and ₹17,999 on Amazon, while [2], the more recent source, gives ₹17,999 on both. It is only worth considering if you find it on sale below ₹15,000.

**Smaller models to skip:** [2] rules out the Qubo Q200, which has a 150 m³/h CADR and is rated for rooms up to 200 sq ft [2][6]. It also says the Levoit Core 300's CADR is on the low side for Delhi winters. Source [1] does not give a CADR for that model, so that is a single-source claim.

**Practical notes:** Insist on True HEPA and avoid "HEPA-type" labels [1][2]. Keep the purifier at least a foot from walls and keep windows closed [1][2]. On smog days, run it on medium or high for an hour, then switch to a quieter setting [2][3].

**Overall confidence:** moderate. All the CADR and price figures come from listings and spec sheets, not independent testing. Prices change often, so confirm before buying.
